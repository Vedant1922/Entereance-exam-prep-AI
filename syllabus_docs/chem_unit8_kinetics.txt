SECTION 1: RATE OF REACTION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Rate of reaction = change in concentration per unit time.

For reaction: aA + bB → cC + dD

   Rate = -1/a × d[A]/dt = -1/b × d[B]/dt = +1/c × d[C]/dt = +1/d × d[D]/dt

→ Negative sign for reactants (concentration decreases with time).
→ Positive sign for products (concentration increases with time).
→ Divided by stoichiometric coefficient to give uniform rate regardless of which species is measured.

Average Rate vs Instantaneous Rate:
→ Average rate = ΔConcentration / Δtime (over a time interval)
→ Instantaneous rate = rate at a specific instant = slope of tangent on concentration-time graph

Units of rate = mol/L/s = M/s = mol L-1 s-1

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: RATE LAW AND ORDER OF REACTION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Rate Law (Rate expression):
   Rate = k[A]^m [B]^n
   [Determined experimentally, NOT from stoichiometric coefficients]

k = Rate constant (specific rate — rate when all concentrations = 1 M)
m = order with respect to A
n = order with respect to B
Overall order = m + n

KEY DISTINCTION:
→ Order of reaction: determined experimentally from rate law.
→ Molecularity: number of molecules/atoms/ions participating in elementary step. Only for elementary reactions. Always a positive integer (1, 2, or 3 rarely).
→ For complex (multi-step) reactions, order ≠ molecularity.
→ Order can be 0, fractional, or negative. Molecularity cannot be fractional or 0.

Units of Rate Constant k:
   Zero order: k has units of mol/L/s = M·s⁻¹
   First order: k has units of s⁻¹ (time⁻¹)
   Second order: k has units of L/mol/s = M⁻¹·s⁻¹
   nth order: k has units of M^(1-n)·s⁻¹

General formula for k units: mol^(1-n) L^(n-1) s^-1
OR: (concentration)^(1-n) × time^(-1)

JEE TRAP: Rate constant k depends on temperature and catalyst, NOT concentration.
Rate depends on concentration. k does not.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3: INTEGRATED RATE LAWS
----------------------------------------------------------------

ZERO ORDER REACTION (rate is independent of concentration):
   Rate = k[A]⁰ = k
   Integrated: [A] = [A]₀ - kt
   Half-life: t½ = [A]₀/2k (depends on initial concentration)
   
   Plot: [A] vs t → straight line, slope = -k

FIRST ORDER REACTION (rate ∝ concentration):
   Rate = k[A]
   Differential: -d[A]/dt = k[A]
   Integrated: ln[A] = ln[A]₀ - kt
   OR: [A] = [A]₀ × e^(-kt)
   OR: log([A]₀/[A]) = kt/2.303
   
   Half-life: t½ = 0.693/k = ln2/k
   [INDEPENDENT of initial concentration — this is characteristic of first order]
   
   Plot: ln[A] or log[A] vs t → straight line, slope = -k/2.303
   
   After n half-lives: [A] = [A]₀ × (1/2)^n

JEE CRITICAL POINT: First order half-life is constant and independent of initial concentration. This means regardless of how much reactant you start with, it takes the same time for half to disappear.

SECOND ORDER REACTION:
   Rate = k[A]²
   Integrated: 1/[A] = 1/[A]₀ + kt
   Half-life: t½ = 1/(k[A]₀) (depends on initial concentration)
   
   Plot: 1/[A] vs t → straight line, slope = k

JEE TRAP: Identifying reaction order from given data:
→ If rate doubles when concentration doubles → first order.
→ If rate quadruples when concentration doubles → second order.
→ If rate doesn't change when concentration changes → zero order.
→ If half-life is constant → first order.

PSEUDO FIRST ORDER REACTIONS:
→ Reactions that appear first order because one reactant is in large excess (concentration essentially constant).
→ Example: Hydrolysis of ester in excess water: CH3COOC2H5 + H2O → CH3COOH + C2H5OH
→ Rate = k[ester][H2O] ≈ k'[ester], where k' = k[H2O] = pseudo first order rate constant.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4: METHODS TO DETERMINE ORDER
----------------------------------------------------------------

Method 1: Initial Rate Method
→ Measure initial rates at different initial concentrations.
→ Keep one reactant concentration constant, vary other.
→ Rate₁/Rate₂ = ([A]₁/[A]₂)^m → solve for m.

Method 2: Graphical Method
→ Plot [A] vs t → straight line if zero order
→ Plot ln[A] vs t → straight line if first order
→ Plot 1/[A] vs t → straight line if second order

Method 3: Half-life Method
→ Constant half-life → first order
→ Half-life increasing with time → second order (depends on decreasing [A])
→ Half-life decreasing with time → zero order

SECTION 5: EFFECT OF TEMPERATURE — ARRHENIUS EQUATION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Rate constant approximately doubles for every 10°C rise in temperature (rough rule of thumb).

Arrhenius Equation:
   k = A × e^(-Ea/RT)
   
   Where:
   A = pre-exponential factor (frequency factor / Arrhenius constant)
   Ea = activation energy (minimum energy required for reaction to occur)
   R = 8.314 J/mol·K
   T = temperature in Kelvin

Logarithmic form:
   ln k = ln A - Ea/RT
   log k = log A - Ea/(2.303 RT)
   
Plot: log k vs 1/T → straight line, slope = -Ea/(2.303R)

Temperature Coefficient (μ) = k(T+10)/k(T) ≈ 2 to 3 for most reactions

Comparing rate constants at two temperatures:
   log(k2/k1) = (Ea/2.303R) × (1/T1 - 1/T2)
   log(k2/k1) = (Ea/2.303R) × (T2-T1)/(T1×T2)

Activation Energy:
→ Minimum energy above average energy that reactant molecules must have to form products.
→ Transition state (activated complex): unstable high-energy intermediate at top of energy barrier.
→ Ea is independent of temperature.
→ Catalyst lowers Ea → increases rate without changing thermodynamics (ΔH, ΔG unaffected).

Exothermic reaction: Ea(forward) < Ea(backward)
Endothermic reaction: Ea(forward) > Ea(backward)
ΔH = Ea(forward) - Ea(backward)

JEE TRAP: Catalyst lowers activation energy for BOTH forward and backward reactions equally. It doesn't change ΔH or equilibrium constant.

Effect of catalyst:
→ Increases k (rate constant) by lowering Ea.
→ Does NOT appear in the overall equation.
→ Does NOT change K (equilibrium constant).
→ Increases rate of both forward and backward reactions equally.

SECTION 6: COLLISION THEORY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Collision theory (qualitative understanding for JEE):

Rate of reaction depends on:
1. Collision frequency (Z): number of collisions per second per unit volume.
   → Increases with concentration and temperature.
2. Threshold energy: minimum kinetic energy required for effective collision.
3. Proper orientation: molecules must collide in correct orientation.

Effective collision = collision with sufficient energy + proper orientation.
   Rate = Z × f × p
   f = fraction of molecules with energy ≥ threshold = e^(-Ea/RT)
   p = probability factor (orientation factor)

Arrhenius A factor = Z × p (combines collision frequency and orientation factor).

At higher temperature:
→ More molecules have kinetic energy ≥ threshold → more effective collisions → higher rate.
→ Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution shifts to higher energies.

JEE TRAP: Not all collisions lead to reaction. Only effective collisions (correct energy + orientation) do.

SECTION 7: JEE-SPECIFIC PATTERNS
----------------------------------------------------------------

HIGH FREQUENCY QUESTION TYPES:
1. Find rate constant k from integrated rate law (given concentration at two times)
2. Calculate half-life and determine order from half-life data
3. Determine order of reaction from initial rate data (method of initial rates)
4. Identify order from concentration-time graph (which plot gives straight line)
5. Calculate Ea using Arrhenius equation at two temperatures
6. Determine units of k for given order
7. Calculate concentration remaining after n half-lives (first order)

CRITICAL TRAPS:
→ For first order: t½ = 0.693/k. For zero order: t½ = [A]₀/2k. For second order: t½ = 1/k[A]₀
→ First order half-life is INDEPENDENT of concentration. Zero and second order are NOT.
→ Order is determined EXPERIMENTALLY. You cannot read it from the balanced equation.
→ Molecularity applies ONLY to elementary steps.
→ Rate of reaction ≠ rate constant k. Rate = k[A]^m[B]^n depends on concentration; k does not.
→ Catalyst lowers Ea but DOES NOT change enthalpy change (ΔH) of reaction.
→ After n half-lives: amount left = (1/2)^n × original amount. Amount reacted = [1 - (1/2)^n] × original.

OUT OF JEE SCOPE:
→ Derivation of Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
→ Detailed collision theory mathematical derivations
→ Chain reactions and free radical mechanisms (in kinetics context)
→ Enzymatic kinetics (Michaelis-Menten)

SECTION 8: KEY FORMULAS SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Rate = k[A]^m[B]^n
[A] = [A]₀ - kt  (zero order)
ln([A]₀/[A]) = kt = 2.303 log([A]₀/[A])  (first order)
1/[A] - 1/[A]₀ = kt  (second order)

t½ = [A]₀/2k  (zero order)
t½ = 0.693/k = ln2/k  (first order, concentration-independent)
t½ = 1/k[A]₀  (second order)

k = Ae^(-Ea/RT)
log(k2/k1) = (Ea/2.303R) × [(T2-T1)/(T1T2)]

Units of k:
Zero order: mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
First order: s⁻¹
Second order: L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
nth order: (mol/L)^(1-n) × s⁻¹
