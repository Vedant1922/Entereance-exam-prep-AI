SECTION 1: ALKANES
----------------------------------------------------------------

General formula: CnH2n+2
All sp³ hybridized carbons, tetrahedral geometry.
Only sigma bonds, no pi bonds.

Nomenclature IUPAC:
→ Find longest chain → number from closest end to substituent → name substituents alphabetically.
→ Methane(C1), Ethane(C2), Propane(C3), Butane(C4), Pentane(C5), Hexane(C6), Heptane(C7), Octane(C8), Nonane(C9), Decane(C10)

Conformations (only for JEE — only ethane tested in detail):
Two types of representations:
→ Newman Projection: look along C-C bond. Front carbon shown as dot, back as circle.
→ Sawhorse Projection: 3D representation showing C-C bond at angle.

Conformations of Ethane:
→ Eclipsed: H's on front and back carbon exactly aligned (dihedral angle 0°). LEAST STABLE (maximum torsional strain).
→ Staggered: H's alternating, maximum separation (dihedral angle 60°). MOST STABLE (minimum strain).
→ Gauche and anti (for butane — gauche has CH3 groups at 60°, anti at 180°). Anti is most stable.

JEE TRAP: Eclipsed conformation has maximum energy (least stable). Staggered is most stable.
These conformations interconvert rapidly at room temperature — NOT isolable isomers.

Reactions of Alkanes:
→ Very unreactive (no functional group, all sigma bonds, nonpolar).
→ Halogenation (most important for JEE):
   Mechanism: FREE RADICAL CHAIN REACTION (requires UV light or heat)
   
   Step 1 INITIATION: X2 → 2X• (homolytic fission by UV light)
   Step 2 PROPAGATION:
   CH4 + X• → CH3• + HX (hydrogen abstraction)
   CH3• + X2 → CH3X + X• (chain continues)
   Step 3 TERMINATION: any two radicals combine.
   
   Reactivity order of halogens: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2 (F2 too reactive, I2 too slow)
   Selectivity order: I2 > Br2 > Cl2 > F2 (Br2 most selective — attacks only 3° C preferentially)
   
JEE TRAP: Chlorination is LESS selective than bromination. Br• is less reactive → more selective → attacks tertiary H preferentially. Cl• is more reactive → less selective → attacks any H.

For propane + Cl2: gives 1-chloropropane AND 2-chloropropane (statistical + selectivity factor).
For propane + Br2: gives mainly 2-bromopropane (bromination is highly selective for 3° and then 2° H).

Combustion: alkanes burn completely to CO2 + H2O (highly exothermic).
Cracking: breaking large alkanes into smaller alkenes (petroleum industry).
Isomerisation: straight chain → branched (increases octane number in petrol).

SECTION 2: ALKENES
----------------------------------------------------------------

General formula: CnH2n (one degree of unsaturation = one C=C)
Contains sp² carbons at double bond.

Preparation (common methods tested in JEE):
→ Dehydration of alcohols with conc. H2SO4 (or Al2O3 at high T): CH3CH2OH → CH2=CH2
→ Dehydrohalogenation of haloalkanes with alcoholic KOH: CH3CH2Br + KOH(alc.) → CH2=CH2
→ Saytzeff's Rule: in elimination, the more substituted alkene (more stable) is the major product.

Geometrical Isomerism of Alkenes:
→ Condition: each C of C=C must have 2 different groups.
→ Cis: same groups on same side. Trans: same groups on opposite sides.
→ E-Z using CIP priority rules: Z = higher priority groups on same side. E = opposite sides.
→ Trans isomer is generally more stable than cis (less steric strain).

Physical Properties:
→ Cis isomer has higher dipole moment than trans (bond dipoles partially cancel in trans).
→ Cis isomer has higher boiling point than trans (due to higher dipole moment → stronger intermolecular forces).
→ Trans isomer has higher melting point than cis (more symmetrical, packs better in crystal).

REACTIONS OF ALKENES:

1. Hydrogenation: C=C + H2 → C-C (catalyst: Ni, Pd, or Pt)
   Heat of hydrogenation: more substituted alkene → less heat → more stable starting material.
   Stability order: 3°(trisubstituted) > 2°(disubstituted) > 1°(monosubstituted) > ethylene.

2. Electrophilic Addition of HX (Markovnikov's Rule):
   In the addition of HX to unsymmetrical alkene, H adds to carbon with MORE hydrogens.
   (OR: X adds to carbon with more alkyl substituents — more stable carbocation intermediate.)
   
   Mechanism (via carbocation):
   Step 1: Proton (H+) from HX adds to double bond → carbocation intermediate (more stable).
   Step 2: X- attacks carbocation → product.
   
   Example: CH3-CH=CH2 + HBr → CH3-CHBr-CH3 (major, Markovnikov) NOT CH3-CH2-CH2Br
   
   Anti-Markovnikov (Peroxide Effect / Kharasch Effect):
   → In presence of peroxides (ROOR) or UV light: HBr adds anti-Markovnikov.
   → Mechanism: FREE RADICAL addition (NOT carbocation).
   → Br• (free radical) adds first to less hindered carbon → carbon radical at more substituted carbon → more stable radical → HBr supplies H to give anti-Markovnikov product.
   
   JEE TRAP: Peroxide effect ONLY applies to HBr. NOT to HCl or HI.
   HCl: even with peroxides, Markovnikov product forms (Cl• is too reactive to be selective).
   HI: even with peroxides, Markovnikov (I• is not reactive enough).

3. Addition of X2 (Halogenation):
   CH2=CH2 + Br2 → CH2Br-CH2Br (1,2-dibromoethane)
   Mechanism: via HALONIUM ION (cyclic bromonium ion intermediate), NOT open carbocation.
   This explains ANTI addition of Br2 to alkene (Br adds from opposite sides).
   
   JEE: Red-brown Br2 solution is decolorized by alkene → test for unsaturation (also alkyne).

4. Addition of Water (Hydration):
   CH2=CH2 + H2O → CH3CH2OH (ethanol) — requires H3PO4 catalyst or dilute H2SO4.
   Follows Markovnikov's rule: OH attaches to more substituted carbon.

5. Ozonolysis:
   C=C + O3 → Ozonide → then reduction (Zn/H2O) or oxidation (H2O2).
   
   Reductive ozonolysis (Zn/H2O):
   → Aldehydes from terminal C=CH2 and C=CHR.
   → Ketones from internal disubstituted carbons (C=CRR').
   
   Oxidative ozonolysis (H2O2 or O3/H2O):
   → Aldehydes → carboxylic acids.
   → Ketones remain as ketones.
   
   JEE APPLICATION: Ozonolysis identifies double bond position (reverse synthesis problem).
   Given: ozonolysis products → find structure of alkene.
   Rule: join the two carbonyl carbons with C=C.

6. Polymerization:
   nCH2=CH2 → (-CH2-CH2-)n (polyethylene) — addition polymerization.

JEE TRAP: For ozonolysis, if reductive cleavage gives CH2O (formaldehyde) → original alkene had terminal =CH2 group. If oxidative cleavage gives CO2 → terminal =CH2 was present.

SECTION 3: ALKYNES
----------------------------------------------------------------

General formula: CnH2n-2 (2 degrees of unsaturation = one C≡C)
Contains sp carbons at triple bond. Linear around triple bond.

Terminal Alkyne (R-C≡C-H): acidic C-H bond (sp carbon, 50% s-character → very electronegative).
→ pKa ≈ 25 (more acidic than alkene ~44 and alkane ~50).
→ Reacts with NaNH2 (sodium amide), Na metal, AgNO3, Cu(I) salts.
→ CH≡CH + AgNO3 + NH3 → Ag-C≡C-Ag (white/yellow precipitate) — test for terminal alkyne.
→ CH≡CH + Cu2Cl2 + NH3 → Cu-C≡C-Cu (red precipitate) — Baeyer's reagent.

Reactions:
1. Hydrogenation: C≡C + H2 → C=C (partial, with Lindlar's catalyst) → C-C (complete, with Ni).
   Lindlar's catalyst (Pd/BaSO4/quinoline): gives cis-alkene (syn addition).
   Na in liquid NH3: gives trans-alkene (anti addition via radical mechanism).

2. Addition of HX: 
   First addition: Markovnikov's rule → vinyl halide.
   Second addition: again Markovnikov → geminal dihalide (both X on same C).
   
3. Addition of X2:
   First addition: trans addition → trans dihaloalkene.
   Second addition → tetrahalide.

4. Hydration (Markovnikov):
   R-C≡CH + H2O (H2SO4, HgSO4) → R-CO-CH3 (ketone) via enol intermediate.
   Except acetylene: CH≡CH + H2O → CH3CHO (acetaldehyde, NOT ketone).
   JEE TRAP: Acetylene hydration gives acetaldehyde (aldehyde), other alkynes give ketones.

5. Polymerization: 
   3CH≡CH → C6H6 (benzene) — at 300°C over activated charcoal (Berthelot reaction).

SECTION 4: AROMATIC HYDROCARBONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Benzene (C6H6) — most important aromatic compound:
→ All 6 C are sp² hybridized, planar hexagonal ring.
→ Each C makes 3 sigma bonds (2 C-C and 1 C-H) + contributes 1 p-orbital perpendicular to ring.
→ 6 p-orbitals overlap to form delocalized π electron cloud (3π bonds delocalized over all 6 C).
→ All C-C bonds equal length (1.40 Å) — intermediate between single (1.54) and double (1.34).

Hückel's Rule for Aromaticity: 4n + 2 π electrons (where n = 0, 1, 2, 3...)
For benzene: 6 π electrons (n=1) → aromatic.
Aromatic systems must also be: cyclic, conjugated (alternating single/double bonds), planar.

Resonance energy of benzene: ~36 kcal/mol (measure of extra stability from delocalization).

JEE EXAMPLES:
Benzene: 6 π e- (n=1) → aromatic. ✓
Cyclooctatetraene (COT): 8 π e- (4×2, doesn't fit 4n+2) → NOT aromatic (antiaromatic if planar).
Naphthalene: 10 π e- (n=2) → aromatic.
Cyclopentadienyl anion C5H5-: 6 π e- (n=1) → aromatic.
Cyclopentadienyl cation C5H5+: 4 π e- → antiaromatic.

ELECTROPHILIC AROMATIC SUBSTITUTION (EAS):
→ Benzene's π electrons act as nucleophile, attract electrophile.
→ Key feature: ring aromaticity is preserved (NOT addition which would destroy aromaticity).

MECHANISM (for all EAS reactions):
Step 1: Generation of electrophile (E+).
Step 2: E+ attacks benzene ring → arenium ion (Wheland intermediate) — sigma complex forms, loses aromaticity temporarily.
Step 3: Loss of H+ (proton) restores aromaticity → substituted benzene.

Types of EAS reactions:

1. HALOGENATION:
   Cl2 + Lewis acid (AlCl3 or FeCl3) → Cl2 → AlCl3 complex → Cl+ (electrophile)
   C6H6 + Cl+ → C6H5Cl (chlorobenzene) + H+
   HCl + AlCl3 complex released.

2. NITRATION:
   HNO3 + H2SO4 (conc.) → NO2+ (nitronium ion) + HSO4- + H2O
   C6H6 + NO2+ → C6H5NO2 (nitrobenzene) + H+
   Temperature control: below 55°C for mononitration; above for dinitration.

3. SULFONATION:
   C6H6 + H2SO4 (fuming, SO3) → C6H5SO3H (benzenesulfonic acid) + H2O
   Reversible reaction (desulfonation by dilute H2SO4 + heat).

4. FRIEDEL-CRAFTS ALKYLATION:
   C6H6 + RX + AlCl3 → C6H5R + HX (Lewis acid catalyst)
   Limitation: polyalkylation occurs (monoalkyl product is more reactive than benzene).
   Limitation: rearrangement of carbocation may change identity of R group.
   
5. FRIEDEL-CRAFTS ACYLATION:
   C6H6 + RCOCl + AlCl3 → C6H5COR + HCl (aryl ketone formed)
   Acyl cation (RCO+, acylium ion) does NOT rearrange (resonance stable).
   No polyacylation (product deactivated by C=O group → less reactive than benzene).
   More useful than alkylation for clean products.

DIRECTIVE INFLUENCE OF SUBSTITUENTS:

ortho/para (o/p) directors: direct incoming electrophile to ortho and para positions.
→ Electron-donating groups activate ring: -OH, -OR, -NH2, -NHR, -NHCOR (activating o/p directors)
→ Halogens: deactivating o/p directors (-I wins over +R for reactivity, but +R wins for position)

meta (m) directors: direct to meta position.
→ Electron-withdrawing groups deactivate ring: -NO2, -CN, -COOH, -CHO, -COR, -SO3H (deactivating m directors)

JEE TRICK to predict directing effect:
→ If substituent can donate electrons to ring by resonance (+R): o/p director.
→ If substituent withdraws electrons from ring by resonance (-R): m director.
→ Halogens: -I effect (deactivating) but +R effect (o/p directing) — check net effect separately for each property.

SUBSTITUTION REACTIONS when TWO substituents present:
→ If both substituents direct to same position: strong orientation in that direction.
→ If they conflict: stronger activating group's directive influence dominates.
→ Strong activating group > weak activating group > deactivating group in directing strength.

JEE TRAP: In o/p directors, statistically more ortho product expected (2 o positions vs 1 p) BUT actually para is often major product (due to less steric crowding at para).

JEE EXAMPLES:
Toluene (CH3 = o/p director): electrophile goes to ortho and para.
Nitrobenzene (NO2 = m director): electrophile goes to meta.
Chlorobenzene (Cl = o/p director but deactivating): electrophile goes to ortho and para, but slower than benzene.
Aniline (NH2 = strongly activating o/p director): electrophile goes to ortho and para, very fast.

HIGH FREQUENCY QUESTION TYPES:
1. Apply Markovnikov's rule to HX/H2O addition to alkene
2. Predict product of radical bromination at 3° position
3. Identify product of ozonolysis (reductive or oxidative)
4. Identify anti-Markovnikov addition (peroxide effect with HBr only)
5. Write product of EAS reaction with correct directing effect
6. Predict major product in Friedel-Crafts reactions
7. Identify cis or trans alkene from Lindlar vs Na/NH3 hydrogenation of alkyne
8. Use acidic character of terminal alkyne for reaction predictions

CRITICAL TRAPS:
→ Peroxide (anti-Markovnikov) effect works ONLY with HBr, NOT HCl or HI.
→ Halogenation of alkanes is free radical. Halogenation of benzene (EAS) is ionic.
→ Br2 decolorization: both alkenes (addition) and alkynes (addition) decolorize Br2/CCl4.
→ Lindlar's catalyst → cis alkene. Na/liquid NH3 → trans alkene.
→ Acetylene + H2O → acetaldehyde. Other terminal alkynes + H2O → ketone.
→ Friedel-Crafts doesn't work with strongly deactivated rings (nitrobenzene, halobenzene is okay but slow).
→ Ozonolysis + Zn/H2O: terminal CH2= gives HCHO (formaldehyde). Oxidative ozonolysis gives CO2 from terminal CH2=.
