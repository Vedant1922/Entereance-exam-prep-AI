SECTION 1: BASIC THERMODYNAMIC TERMS
----------------------------------------------------------------

System: The part of universe under study.
Surroundings: Everything else outside the system.
Boundary: Separates system from surroundings.

Types of Systems:
→ Open system: Exchanges both matter AND energy with surroundings. (e.g., open beaker)
→ Closed system: Exchanges only energy, NOT matter. (e.g., sealed gas in a cylinder)
→ Isolated system: Exchanges NEITHER matter nor energy. (e.g., thermos flask — ideal)

Types of Processes:
→ Isothermal: constant temperature (ΔT = 0)
→ Adiabatic: no heat exchange (q = 0)
→ Isobaric: constant pressure (ΔP = 0)
→ Isochoric: constant volume (ΔV = 0)

State Functions vs Path Functions:
→ State function: depends only on initial and final state, NOT the path taken.
   Examples: U (internal energy), H (enthalpy), S (entropy), G (Gibbs energy), T, P, V
→ Path function: depends on the path taken.
   Examples: q (heat), w (work)

Extensive vs Intensive Properties:
→ Extensive: depends on amount of matter. Examples: mass, volume, U, H, S, G
→ Intensive: does not depend on amount. Examples: T, P, density, molar quantities, concentration

SECTION 2: INTERNAL ENERGY AND FIRST LAW
----------------------------------------------------------------

Internal Energy (U):
→ Total energy stored within a system (kinetic + potential of all molecules).
→ Is a state function — only ΔU matters, not absolute value.
→ For ideal gas: U depends only on temperature (not P or V).

First Law of Thermodynamics:
   ΔU = q + w
   [Energy is conserved — change in internal energy = heat absorbed + work done ON system]

Sign Convention (IUPAC — very important for JEE):
→ q > 0: heat absorbed by system (endothermic)
→ q < 0: heat released by system (exothermic)
→ w > 0: work done ON system (compression)
→ w < 0: work done BY system (expansion)

Work of Expansion/Compression:
   w = -Pext × ΔV = -Pext (V2 - V1)
   [Negative sign because expansion (ΔV > 0) means work done BY system]

For Free Expansion (into vacuum): Pext = 0 → w = 0
For Isothermal Reversible Expansion of ideal gas:
   w = -nRT ln(V2/V1) = -2.303 nRT log(V2/V1)
For Adiabatic process: q = 0, so ΔU = w

JEE TRAP: ΔU for isothermal expansion of ideal gas = 0 (because ΔT = 0 for ideal gas).
So for isothermal: q = -w (heat absorbed = work done by gas)

SECTION 3: ENTHALPY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Enthalpy (H):
   H = U + PV
   ΔH = ΔU + Δ(PV)

At constant pressure: ΔH = qp (enthalpy change = heat at constant pressure)
At constant volume: ΔU = qv (internal energy change = heat at constant volume)

Relationship between ΔH and ΔU for gaseous reactions:
   ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT
   Δng = moles of gaseous products - moles of gaseous reactants
   R = 8.314 J/mol·K

JEE EXAMPLE: N2(g) + 3H2(g) → 2NH3(g)
   Δng = 2 - (1+3) = -2
   ΔH = ΔU + (-2)RT = ΔU - 2RT
   [ΔH < ΔU for this reaction]

Sign convention:
→ Exothermic reactions: ΔH < 0 (heat released, products more stable)
→ Endothermic reactions: ΔH > 0 (heat absorbed)

Heat Capacity:
→ C = q/ΔT (heat required per unit temperature rise)
→ Specific heat capacity (c): per gram. Molar heat capacity (Cm): per mole.
→ For ideal gases: Cp - Cv = R (= 8.314 J/mol·K)
→ Cp > Cv always (extra work done against pressure at constant P)
→ γ = Cp/Cv (ratio of heat capacities)

Enthalpy of Reaction = Σ(enthalpy of bonds broken) - Σ(enthalpy of bonds formed)
Or: ΔH°rxn = Σ ΔHf°(products) - Σ ΔHf°(reactants)

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4: STANDARD ENTHALPIES — ALL TYPES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Standard conditions: 298 K, 1 bar pressure, 1 M concentration.

Standard Enthalpy of Formation (ΔHf°):
→ Enthalpy change when 1 mole of compound is formed from elements in standard states.
→ ΔHf° of any element in standard state = 0 (by definition)
→ Example: ΔHf° of H2O(l) = -286 kJ/mol

Standard Enthalpy of Combustion (ΔHc°):
→ Enthalpy change when 1 mole of substance burns completely in O2.
→ Always negative (combustion is exothermic).
→ Products: CO2(g) for C, H2O(l) for H, SO2(g) for S.

Standard Enthalpy of Atomization (ΔHatom):
→ Enthalpy to convert 1 mole of substance to gaseous atoms.
→ For diatomic: equals half the bond dissociation enthalpy.
→ Always positive (endothermic — bonds must be broken).

Bond Dissociation Enthalpy:
→ Enthalpy to break 1 mole of specific bond in gaseous state.
→ Always positive (endothermic).
→ ΔHrxn = Σ(bonds broken) - Σ(bonds formed)
→ Positive values = energy input, so: ΔH = ΣBE(reactants) - ΣBE(products)

Lattice Enthalpy:
→ Enthalpy for formation of ionic crystal from gaseous ions.
→ Calculated via Born-Haber cycle (Hess's law applied to ionic compounds).

Enthalpy of Solution:
→ ΔHsoln = ΔHlattice + ΔHhydration
→ If ΔHhydration > ΔHlattice: exothermic dissolution
→ If ΔHhydration < ΔHlattice: endothermic dissolution (e.g., NH4NO3 dissolving in water is endothermic)

Enthalpy of Neutralization:
→ For strong acid + strong base: always -57.1 kJ/mol
→ For weak acid or weak base: less than 57.1 kJ/mol (some energy used to ionize weak acid/base)

SECTION 5: HESS'S LAW
----------------------------------------------------------------

Hess's Law: ΔH for overall reaction = sum of ΔH for individual steps.
(Because H is a state function — path doesn't matter)

Method: Add thermochemical equations like algebraic equations.
→ If you reverse a reaction, reverse the sign of ΔH.
→ If you multiply a reaction by n, multiply ΔH by n.

JEE APPLICATION: Used to calculate:
→ Enthalpy of reactions that cannot be measured directly.
→ Lattice enthalpy via Born-Haber cycle.
→ Bond dissociation enthalpies.

SECTION 6: SECOND LAW AND ENTROPY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Second Law: Spontaneous processes increase the entropy of the universe.
   ΔSuniverse = ΔSsystem + ΔSsurroundings ≥ 0

Entropy (S):
→ Measure of disorder or randomness in a system.
→ State function: only ΔS matters.
→ S increases with: heating, phase change (solid→liquid→gas), dissolution, more moles of gas.

   ΔS = qrev/T (at constant temperature)
   qrev = heat absorbed in reversible process

Sign of ΔS:
→ Positive: disorder increases (melting, vaporization, dissolution, more gas moles in products)
→ Negative: disorder decreases (crystallization, condensation, fewer gas moles in products)

Predicting ΔS sign for reactions:
→ If Δng(gas) > 0: ΔS > 0 (more moles of gas → more disorder)
→ If Δng(gas) < 0: ΔS < 0

JEE EXAMPLE: CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g) → Δng = +1 → ΔS > 0

Third Law of Thermodynamics:
→ Entropy of perfectly crystalline substance at absolute zero (0 K) = 0.
→ This defines the absolute scale of entropy (unlike U and H where we can only measure changes).

SECTION 7: GIBBS FREE ENERGY AND SPONTANEITY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Gibbs Free Energy (G):
   G = H - TS
   ΔG = ΔH - TΔS (at constant T and P)

Criteria for Spontaneity:
   ΔG < 0: Spontaneous (favorable) process
   ΔG > 0: Non-spontaneous (requires input of energy)
   ΔG = 0: System at equilibrium (no net change)

SPONTANEITY TABLE — JEE LOVES THESE QUESTIONS:
ΔH | ΔS | ΔG = ΔH - TΔS | Spontaneity
-  |  + | Always negative | Always spontaneous (at all temperatures)
+  |  - | Always positive | Never spontaneous (non-spontaneous at all temperatures)
-  |  - | Depends on T    | Spontaneous at LOW temperature (when |ΔH| > |TΔS|)
+  |  + | Depends on T    | Spontaneous at HIGH temperature (when TΔS > ΔH)

JEE EXAMPLE: Melting of ice: ΔH > 0 (endothermic), ΔS > 0 (disorder increases)
→ Spontaneous above 0°C (high T favors). At 0°C, ΔG = 0 (equilibrium).

Relationship between ΔG° and Equilibrium Constant:
   ΔG° = -RT ln K = -2.303 RT log K
   If ΔG° < 0: K > 1 (products favored at equilibrium)
   If ΔG° > 0: K < 1 (reactants favored at equilibrium)
   If ΔG° = 0: K = 1

Relationship between ΔG° and cell potential (electrochemistry link):
   ΔG° = -nFE°cell
   [n = moles of electrons, F = Faraday's constant = 96500 C/mol]

Work Function:
→ Maximum non-PV work = -ΔG (at constant T and P)
→ This is why ΔG is called "free energy" — it's the energy free/available to do work.

SECTION 8: JEE-SPECIFIC PATTERNS

HIGH FREQUENCY QUESTION TYPES:
1. Calculate ΔH using Hess's law or bond energies
2. Predict sign of ΔS for given reactions
3. Determine temperature range for spontaneity given ΔH and ΔS

5. Relate ΔG° to equilibrium constant K
6. Calculate ΔH vs ΔU using ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT
7. Born-Haber cycle numerical for lattice enthalpy

CRITICAL TRAPS:
→ ΔU = 0 for isothermal expansion of ideal gas (NOT for real gases)
→ ΔHcombustion is always negative (combustion = exothermic). If answer is positive, check signs.
→ ΔHf° of elements in standard state = 0. Don't assign values to H2(g), O2(g), C(graphite), etc.
→ Work = -PextΔV. For expansion, ΔV is positive, so w is negative (work done BY system).
→ In bond energy calculations: energy to break bonds is always positive (endothermic).
→ Standard state of water for ΔHcombustion is liquid H2O(l) NOT H2O(g).
