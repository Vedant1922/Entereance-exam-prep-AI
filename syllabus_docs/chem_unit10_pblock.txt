SECTION 1: GROUP 13 — BORON FAMILY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Elements: B, Al, Ga, In, Tl
Configuration: ns² np¹ → 3 valence electrons, maximum valence = 3

First Element Uniqueness (Boron):
→ B is a metalloid (semi-metal), not a metal (unlike Al, Ga, In, Tl which are metals).
→ B forms only covalent compounds (no B3+ ion due to high charge density).
→ Al forms both ionic (AlCl3 is partially covalent by Fajan's rules) and covalent.
→ B has tendency to form three-center two-electron bonds (found in diboranes, B2H6).

Key Trends:
→ Atomic radius increases down the group.
→ IE decreases down the group.
→ Metallic character increases down the group (B → non-metal, Al-Tl → metals).
→ +1 oxidation state becomes more stable down the group (inert pair effect: Tl is mostly +1).

Important Compounds:
Borax: Na2B4O7·10H2O (sodium tetraborate)
→ Used in soap, cleaning, as flux in metallurgy.
→ Borax bead test: identifies metal ions by characteristic flame color.

Boric acid: B(OH)3 or H3BO3
→ Weak monobasic acid (Lewis acid, not Bronsted acid — accepts OH- rather than donating H+).

Boron Trifluoride (BF3):
→ sp² hybridized, trigonal planar, Lewis acid (accepts electron pairs).
→ Completes octet by acting as Lewis acid with Lewis bases like NH3, F-.

Alums: M+M3+(SO4)2·12H2O (e.g., KAl(SO4)2·12H2O — potash alum)
→ Double salts. Used in water purification (Al3+ causes coagulation of colloidal impurities).

JEE TRAP: BF3 is sp² hybridized (not sp³). It's an electron-deficient compound — a Lewis acid.

Diagonal Relationship (B and Si):
→ B resembles Si more than it resembles Al. Both form acidic oxides, covalent compounds, similar type of hydrides.

SECTION 2: GROUP 14 — CARBON FAMILY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Elements: C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb
Configuration: ns² np² → 4 valence electrons, maximum valence = 4

First Element Uniqueness (Carbon):
→ Carbon has unique ability to form long chains (catenation) — basis of entire organic chemistry.
→ No other element in Group 14 can catenate to the same extent.
→ C-C bond is strong and stable. Si-Si bond is weaker.

Allotropes of Carbon: Graphite, Diamond, Fullerene (Buckminster fullerene, C60), Carbon nanotubes.
→ Diamond: sp³, tetrahedral network, hardest natural substance, poor conductor.
→ Graphite: sp², hexagonal layers, layers held by van der Waals forces, good conductor (delocalized π electrons).
→ Fullerene: sp², C60 soccer ball structure, cage-like.

Silicon:
→ Si is semiconductor, forms SiO2 (silica), silicates, silicones.
→ SiO2 is network covalent solid (not molecular like CO2) — Si needs expanded octet.
→ Why Si doesn't form SiO2 as gas like CO2? Because Si cannot form pπ-pπ double bonds (no π bonding in Si=O).

Inert Pair Effect (down the group):
→ Sn shows +2 and +4 states. Pb mostly +2 (Pb2+ more stable than Pb4+).
→ PbO2 (Pb4+) is a strong oxidizing agent (unstable, wants to become Pb2+).

Carbon Oxides:
→ CO: toxic (binds hemoglobin), reducing agent, Lewis base (has lone pair on C).
→ CO2: Lewis acid (reacts with Lewis bases like OH- to form carbonate).

JEE TRAP: CO is a Lewis base (donates lone pair from C). CO2 is a Lewis acid (accepts electron pair — reacts with NaOH to give Na2CO3).

SECTION 3: GROUP 15 — NITROGEN FAMILY (PNICTOGENS)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Elements: N, P, As, Sb, Bi
Configuration: ns² np³ → 5 valence electrons
Maximum oxidation state: +5. Minimum: -3.

First Element Uniqueness (Nitrogen):
→ N forms multiple bonds (N≡N, N=N, N=O). Due to small size and availability of p orbitals for π bonding.
→ N has lone pair but does NOT form d-orbitals (unlike P, As, Sb which have d-orbitals).
→ N cannot form NF5 (no d-orbitals to expand octet). P can form PCl5 (uses d-orbitals).
→ N2 is extremely stable (triple bond, ΔHf = 0, ΔGf = 0).
→ Fixation of N2 (converting to usable forms): Haber process (industrial), bacteria (biological).

Nitrogen Compounds for JEE:
→ NH3 (ammonia): sp³, pyramidal, Lewis base, manufactured by Haber process.
→ N2O4: colorless gas ⇌ NO2 (brown paramagnetic gas), equilibrium tested in JEE.
→ HNO3: strong acid, oxidizing agent. Reacts with Cu (giving NO2 with conc., NO with dilute).
→ HNO2: weak acid, used as diazotizing agent.

Phosphorus allotropes:
→ White phosphorus (P4): tetrahedral, toxic, reactive, waxy solid.
→ Red phosphorus: polymer, less reactive, used in match boxes.
→ Black phosphorus: most stable, semiconducting.

PCl3 (sp³, pyramidal) vs PCl5 (sp³d, trigonal bipyramidal):
→ PCl3 + Cl2 → PCl5 (phosphorus expands octet using d-orbitals).
→ Nitrogen CANNOT form NCl5 (no d-orbitals).

Phosphorus Oxoacids (important for JEE):
→ H3PO4 (orthophosphoric acid): triprotic, +5 oxidation state.
→ H3PO3 (phosphorous acid): diprotic only (one P-H bond, non-ionizable), +3 oxidation state.
→ H3PO2 (hypophosphorous acid): monoprotic only, +1 oxidation state.
→ RULE: Number of ionizable H = number of P-OH groups. P-H bonds are non-ionizable.
→ H3PO3 is a strong reducing agent (has P-H bond, lower oxidation state).

JEE TRAP: H3PO3 has 3 H atoms but is DIPROTIC (only 2 OH groups; 1 H is directly bonded to P).

SECTION 4: GROUP 16 — OXYGEN FAMILY (CHALCOGENS)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Elements: O, S, Se, Te, Po
Configuration: ns² np⁴ → 6 valence electrons
Oxidation states: -2, -1, +2, +4, +6

First Element Uniqueness (Oxygen):
→ O only forms -2 oxidation state (except peroxides -1, OF2 +2, superoxides -½).
→ O cannot expand octet (no d-orbitals in Period 2).
→ O forms very strong hydrogen bonds and stable O=O double bond in O2.
→ S can form SF6 (expands octet). O cannot form OF6.
→ O is more electronegative than S, so O-H bonds are more polar → stronger hydrogen bonding.

Allotropes of Sulfur:
→ Rhombic sulfur (α-S): stable below 96°C, crown structure S8 rings.
→ Monoclinic sulfur (β-S): stable above 96°C, also S8 rings.
→ Transition temperature: 96°C (interconversion point).
→ Plastic sulfur: amorphous, formed by pouring boiling S into cold water.

Oxoacids of Sulfur (important):
→ H2SO4 (sulfuric acid): strong diprotic acid, dehydrating agent, oxidizing agent.
→ H2SO3 (sulfurous acid): weak, +4 state, reducing agent.
→ H2S2O7 (pyrosulfuric acid / oleum): formed by SO3 dissolving in H2SO4.
→ H2S2O8 (peroxodisulfuric acid / Marshall's acid): strongest oxidizing oxoacid of S.
→ Na2S2O3 (sodium thiosulfate): used as anti-chlor (removes excess Cl2), reacts with I2.

Ozone (O3):
→ Bent shape, sp² hybridized, resonance structure.
→ Powerful oxidizing agent (stronger than O2).
→ Decomposes to O2 + O (atomic oxygen = actual oxidizing agent).
→ Formed in upper atmosphere from O2 by UV radiation.
→ Absorbs harmful UV radiation (protects life on Earth).
→ CFC compounds deplete ozone layer.

JEE TRAP: Ozone is a better oxidizing agent than O2. Tests: turns starch-iodide paper blue (releases I2 from KI), turns moist litmus red (unlike O2 which doesn't affect it).

SECTION 5: GROUP 17 — HALOGENS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Elements: F, Cl, Br, I, At
Configuration: ns² np⁵ → 7 valence electrons, need 1 electron to complete octet.
Most electronegative group.

First Element Uniqueness (Fluorine):
→ F has only -1 oxidation state (most electronegative, cannot be oxidized further).
→ F cannot form positive oxidation states (no d-orbitals available for expansion).
→ F forms strongest H-bond (F-H···F).
→ HF is a WEAK acid (despite F being most electronegative) because F-H bond is very strong (high bond dissociation energy makes ionization difficult).
→ HCl, HBr, HI are strong acids. Only HF is weak.

Acid strength order: HF << HCl < HBr < HI
(Bond strength: HF > HCl > HBr > HI → Stronger bond = harder to ionize = weaker acid)

Oxidizing power order: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2 (decreases down group, E° becomes less positive)
Reducing power of halide ions: I- > Br- > Cl- > F- (I- is best reducing agent)

Hydrogen Halide bond strength: HF > HCl > HBr > HI (decreasing down group)
Boiling point: HF > HI > HBr > HCl (HF has H-bonding, anomaly — normally BP increases down)

Interhalogen Compounds: formed between two different halogens.
→ XX' type: ClF, BrF, ICl, IBr
→ XX'3 type: ClF3, BrF3, IF3
→ XX'5 type: BrF5, IF5
→ XX'7 type: IF7 (only one of this type)
→ The lighter halogen is in the center for higher compounds: IF7 has I as center.
→ They are more reactive than parent halogens.

Oxoacids of Halogens (for Cl, Br, I):
→ HOCl (hypochlorous acid, +1): weakest, best bleach/germicide.
→ HClO2 (chlorous acid, +3)
→ HClO3 (chloric acid, +5)
→ HClO4 (perchloric acid, +7): strongest acid among oxoacids.
→ Acid strength increases with oxidation state: HOCl < HClO2 < HClO3 < HClO4.
→ Oxidizing power: HOCl > HClO2 > HClO3 > HClO4 (opposite to acid strength).

JEE TRAP: HClO4 (perchloric acid) is the strongest acid among all oxoacids. It's in the same league as strong acids like H2SO4.

Chlorine (Cl2) reactions:
→ With water: Cl2 + H2O ⇌ HCl + HOCl (disproportionation)
→ With NaOH (cold dilute): Cl2 + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaOCl + H2O (sodium hypochlorite — bleaching agent)
→ With NaOH (hot conc.): 3Cl2 + 6NaOH → 5NaCl + NaClO3 + 3H2O (sodium chlorate)

Bleaching Powder: Ca(OCl)Cl or Ca(ClO)Cl
→ Mixture of calcium hypochlorite and chloride.
→ Bleaching due to OCl- which releases Cl2 in presence of acid (moisture + CO2).

JEE TRAP: Bleaching powder is NOT pure Ca(OCl)2. It's the mixed salt Ca(OCl)Cl.

SECTION 6: GROUP 18 — NOBLE GASES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Elements: He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn
Configuration: ns² np⁶ (completely filled valence shell)
→ Chemically inert (stable octet or duplet for He).
→ Monoatomic, non-reactive under normal conditions.

Xenon compounds (Kr can also form few):
→ XeF2, XeF4, XeF6 (fluorides), XeO3, XeOF4
→ XeF2: linear, sp³d hybridization (3 lone pairs).
→ XeF4: square planar, sp³d² hybridization (2 lone pairs).
→ XeF6: distorted octahedral, sp³d³ hybridization (1 lone pair).

JEE TRAP: Even noble gases can form compounds (with highly electronegative F and O under high pressure). Xe compounds are more stable than Kr compounds.

Applications: He (balloons, diving mixtures), Ne (neon signs), Ar (inert atmosphere in metallurgy, light bulbs), Kr and Xe (photography, lighting).

HIGH FREQUENCY QUESTION TYPES:
1. Compare properties within a group or across period for p-block elements
2. Identify number of ionizable H in phosphorus oxoacids
3. Identify oxidation state of element in compound
4. Predict product of halogen reaction with NaOH (hot vs cold)
5. Identify allotropes and their properties (carbon, sulfur, phosphorus)
6. Compare acid strength of hydrohalic acids or oxoacids
7. XeF compound shapes using VSEPR
8. Explain why first element differs from rest of group

CRITICAL TRAPS:
→ H3PO3 is diprotic (not triprotic). H3PO2 is monoprotic.
→ HF is a weak acid (NOT strong despite F being most electronegative).
→ Acid strength of hydrohalic acids: HI > HBr > HCl > HF (weakest).
→ Acid strength of oxoacids of Cl: HClO4 > HClO3 > HClO2 > HOCl.
→ Bleaching powder formula: Ca(OCl)Cl (not Ca(ClO)2).
→ N cannot form NCl5 (no d-orbitals). P can form PCl5.
→ CO is Lewis base (lone pair on C). CO2 is Lewis acid.
→ Cl2 + cold NaOH → NaOCl. Cl2 + hot NaOH → NaClO3.
