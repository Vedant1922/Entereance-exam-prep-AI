SECTION 1: MODERN PERIODIC LAW AND TABLE STRUCTURE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Modern Periodic Law (Moseley): Properties of elements are periodic functions of their ATOMIC NUMBER (not atomic mass).

Structure of the Periodic Table:
→ 7 Periods (horizontal rows), 18 Groups (vertical columns)
→ Period number = number of principal quantum shells occupied.

Period 1: 2 elements (H, He) — fills 1s
Period 2: 8 elements (Li to Ne) — fills 2s, 2p
Period 3: 8 elements (Na to Ar) — fills 3s, 3p
Period 4: 18 elements (K to Kr) — fills 4s, 3d, 4p
Period 5: 18 elements (Rb to Xe) — fills 5s, 4d, 5p
Period 6: 32 elements — fills 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p
Period 7: 32 elements — fills 7s, 5f, 6d, 7p

Block Classification (based on which subshell is being filled):
→ s-block: Groups 1 and 2 (Li, Na, K... and Be, Mg, Ca...) — filling s orbital
→ p-block: Groups 13-18 (B, C, N... to He/Ne/Ar...) — filling p orbital
→ d-block: Groups 3-12 (transition metals, Sc to Zn, etc.) — filling d orbital
→ f-block: Lanthanoids and Actinoids (inner transition elements) — filling f orbital

JEE TRAP: H and He are unique.
→ H is placed in Group 1 (like alkali metals) but also resembles halogens. It's neither a metal nor non-metal.
→ He is in Group 18 (noble gases) but is an s-block element.

Group Naming (old IUPAC):
Group 1: Alkali metals (Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr)
Group 2: Alkaline earth metals (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra)
Group 17: Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I, At)
Group 18: Noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn)

SECTION 2: PERIODIC TRENDS IN PROPERTIES
----------------------------------------------------------------

ATOMIC RADIUS:
→ Defined as half the internuclear distance between identical neighboring atoms.
→ Covalent radius: half of bond length in diatomic molecule (for non-metals).
→ Van der Waals radius: half of internuclear distance between non-bonded atoms.
→ Van der Waals > Covalent radius.

Trend across period (left to right):
→ Atomic radius DECREASES.
→ Reason: nuclear charge (Z) increases, electrons added to same shell — increased Zeff pulls electrons closer.

Trend down group (top to bottom):
→ Atomic radius INCREASES.
→ Reason: new shell added, electrons farther from nucleus despite more protons.

Exceptions:
→ Noble gases have very large van der Waals radii (appear to break the trend in groups, but comparing different types of radii).

IONIC RADIUS:
→ Cation (M+) < parent atom (electrons removed → less shielding → nucleus pulls remaining harder)
→ Anion (X-) > parent atom (extra electrons added → more repulsion → expanded)

Isoelectronic species (same number of electrons, different Z):
→ Higher nuclear charge = smaller radius.
→ Order: N3- > O2- > F- > Ne > Na+ > Mg2+ > Al3+
→ All have 10 electrons, but Z increases → more attraction → smaller size.

JEE TRAP: Along isoelectronic series: higher Z → SMALLER size (more protons pulling same number of electrons).

----------------------------------------------------------------

IONIZATION ENTHALPY (Ionization Energy, IE):
→ Minimum energy required to remove outermost electron from neutral gaseous atom.
→ M(g) → M+(g) + e-   ΔH = IE1 (first ionization energy)
→ M+(g) → M2+(g) + e-  ΔH = IE2 (second IE)
→ Always positive (endothermic — energy must be supplied).
→ IE2 > IE1 always (harder to remove electron from positive ion).

Trend across period:
→ IE generally INCREASES left to right.
→ Higher Zeff, smaller radius → more tightly bound electrons → harder to remove.

Exceptions in 2nd period:
→ Be (Z=4) > B (Z=5): B has one 2p electron which is more shielded by 2s electrons and easier to remove.
   IE order: Li < B < Be (Be doesn't follow the expected increase — its 2s² is harder to remove than B's 2p¹)
→ N (Z=7) > O (Z=8): N has half-filled 2p³ (extra stability, symmetric). O has 2p⁴ (paired electrons repel each other), so O has lower IE than N.
   IE order: N > O (anomaly — half-filled 2p is extra stable)

MUST MEMORIZE FOR JEE:
In Period 2: Li < B < Be < C < O < N < F < Ne
(Two major exceptions: Be > B, and N > O)

Trend down group:
→ IE DECREASES (atomic radius increases → outer electrons farther from nucleus → easier to remove).

----------------------------------------------------------------

ELECTRON GAIN ENTHALPY (Electron Affinity):
→ Energy change when electron is added to neutral gaseous atom.
→ X(g) + e- → X-(g) ΔH = ΔegH
→ Can be negative (energy released) or positive (energy required).

Trend across period:
→ Generally becomes more negative (more energy released) from left to right.
→ Non-metals readily accept electrons.

Exceptions:
→ F (fluorine) has lower (less negative) electron gain enthalpy than Cl despite being more electronegative.
   Reason: F is extremely small — electron-electron repulsion in compact 2p orbital is high when adding electron.
   Cl > F in electron gain enthalpy: Cl(-349) > F(-328) kJ/mol
→ Noble gases: very high positive electron gain enthalpy (resist gaining electrons).
→ N, Be, Mg have positive (unfavorable) electron gain enthalpy (stable configurations).

CRITICAL JEE FACT: Cl has the highest electron gain enthalpy among halogens (NOT fluorine).

Trend down group:
→ Electron gain enthalpy becomes less negative (less energy released) going down the group.

----------------------------------------------------------------

ELECTRONEGATIVITY:
→ Ability of bonded atom to attract shared electron pair toward itself.
→ Pauling scale: F = 4.0 (highest), Cs = 0.7 (lowest among stable elements).

Trend across period: INCREASES (left to right)
Trend down group: DECREASES

Order: F > O > N ≈ Cl > Br > C > S > I > H > P...

JEE APPLICATION:
→ Higher electronegativity difference → more ionic bond character.
→ Electronegativity determines polarity of bonds and direction of dipole moment.
→ Pauling used electronegativity to explain bond character in Fajan's rules.

SECTION 3: VALENCE AND OXIDATION STATES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Valence = combining capacity of element.
Most elements show valence = group number or (18 - group number) for p-block.

Common Oxidation States for JEE:
→ Group 1: +1 only
→ Group 2: +2 only
→ Group 13: +3 (and +1 for heavier elements — inert pair effect)
→ Group 14: +4, +2 (inert pair effect increases down group)
→ Group 15: -3, +3, +5
→ Group 16: -2, +4, +6
→ Group 17: -1, +1, +3, +5, +7 (F only -1, no +ve states for F)
→ Group 18: 0 (Kr, Xe can be +2, +4 in compounds — but rarely asked)

Inert Pair Effect: In heavier p-block elements (Tl, Pb, Bi), +2 lower oxidation state becomes increasingly stable because the s-electron pair resists participation in bonding.
→ Tl (+1 > +3), Pb (+2 > +4), Bi (+3 > +5) are more stable lower states.

SECTION 4: JEE-SPECIFIC PATTERNS
----------------------------------------------------------------

HIGH FREQUENCY QUESTION TYPES:
1. Identify block, period, group of element given atomic number or configuration
2. Compare atomic/ionic radii of given species (including isoelectronic)
3. Arrange elements in increasing/decreasing order of IE
4. Identify exception in periodic trend (N>O, Cl>F in electron affinity, Be>B)
5. Compare electronegativity and predict bond polarity
6. Identify inert pair effect in heavier p-block elements

CRITICAL TRAPS:
→ F has highest electronegativity but NOT the highest electron gain enthalpy (Cl does).
→ N > O in ionization enthalpy (half-filled stability of N's 2p³).
→ Be > B in ionization enthalpy (filled 2s² vs empty 2p).
→ Ionic radius: cation is SMALLER than parent atom. Anion is LARGER.
→ In isoelectronic series: MORE protons = SMALLER radius (more attraction with same electrons).
→ IE2 is always greater than IE1 for same atom.

SECTION 5: KEY RELATIONSHIPS SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------

Across period (L to R): Atomic radius ↓, IE ↑, EN ↑, EA more negative
Down group: Atomic radius ↑, IE ↓, EN ↓
Cation < parent atom < Anion (size comparison)
Isoelectronic: higher Z = smaller size
Exceptions: Be > B (IE), N > O (IE), Cl > F (electron gain enthalpy)
F has highest EN (4.0 Pauling scale)
Cl has highest electron gain enthalpy
