SECTION 1: OXIDATION AND REDUCTION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Classical definitions:
→ Oxidation: addition of oxygen, removal of hydrogen, or loss of electrons.
→ Reduction: removal of oxygen, addition of hydrogen, or gain of electrons.

Electronic concept (JEE level):
→ Oxidation: LOSS of electrons (OIL: Oxidation Is Loss)
→ Reduction: GAIN of electrons (RIG: Reduction Is Gain)
→ "OIL RIG" — memorize this.

Oxidizing agent (oxidant): accepts electrons, gets reduced.
Reducing agent (reductant): donates electrons, gets oxidized.

In every redox reaction: substance oxidized = reducing agent, substance reduced = oxidizing agent.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: OXIDATION NUMBER (OXIDATION STATE)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Rules for assigning oxidation number (in order of priority):

1. Pure elements: oxidation number = 0 (Na, Fe, O2, N2, S8 etc.)
2. Monatomic ions: oxidation number = ionic charge (Na+ = +1, Cl- = -1, Al3+ = +3)
3. Hydrogen: +1 in most compounds, EXCEPT -1 in metal hydrides (NaH, CaH2, LiAlH4)
4. Oxygen: -2 in most compounds, EXCEPT:
   → -1 in peroxides (H2O2, Na2O2, BaO2)
   → -½ in superoxides (KO2)
   → +2 in OF2 (oxygen is more electronegative than F? No! F is MORE electronegative — O is +2)
5. Fluorine: ALWAYS -1 (most electronegative element)
6. Sum of all oxidation numbers in neutral compound = 0
7. Sum of all oxidation numbers in ion = charge of ion

JEE EXAMPLES:
→ In Cr2O7²-: 2x + 7(-2) = -2 → x = +6 (Cr is +6)
→ In MnO4-: x + 4(-2) = -1 → x = +7 (Mn is +7)
→ In SO4²-: x + 4(-2) = -2 → x = +6 (S is +6)
→ In Na2S2O3: 2(+1) + 2x + 3(-2) = 0 → x = +2 (average, S has +2 oxidation state on average)
→ In S4O6²-: average S = +2.5 (fractional oxidation state is possible)

JEE TRAP: In H2O2, O is -1 (peroxide). In OF2, O is +2 (F is -1, not O). 
In Mn2O7, Mn is +7 (not +5 as some students calculate — check: 2(+7) + 7(-2) = 0 ✓).

Disproportionation: When the same element simultaneously gets oxidized and reduced.
Example: Cl2 + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaOCl + H2O
Cl goes from 0 to -1 (in NaCl, reduced) and 0 to +1 (in NaOCl, oxidized).

SECTION 3: BALANCING REDOX REACTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Two methods: Oxidation number method and Half-reaction (ion-electron) method.

Half-Reaction Method (most used in JEE):

Step 1: Split into two half-reactions (oxidation and reduction).
Step 2: Balance atoms other than H and O.
Step 3: Balance O by adding H2O.
Step 4: Balance H by adding H+.
Step 5: Balance charge by adding electrons.
Step 6: Multiply half-reactions to equalize electrons transferred.
Step 7: Add half-reactions and cancel common terms.
Step 8: In basic solution: add OH- to both sides to neutralize H+, form H2O.

JEE EXAMPLE: MnO4- + Fe2+ in acidic solution:
Oxidation: Fe2+ → Fe3+ + e-
Reduction: MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e- → Mn2+ + 4H2O
Multiply oxidation by 5: 5Fe2+ → 5Fe3+ + 5e-
Net: MnO4- + 5Fe2+ + 8H+ → Mn2+ + 5Fe3+ + 4H2O

SECTION 4: ELECTROLYTIC AND METALLIC CONDUCTION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Metallic Conduction:
→ Flow of electrons through metal conductor.
→ Conductor: substance that conducts electricity.
→ Resistance (R) in ohms (Ω), Conductance (G) = 1/R in siemens (S)

Electrolytic Conduction:
→ Flow of ions through electrolyte solution.
→ Requires liquid medium (aqueous solution or molten salt).
→ Increases with temperature (more ionic mobility, less viscosity).

Specific Conductance (κ, kappa):
   κ = (1/R) × (l/A) = G × (l/A)
   [l = length between electrodes, A = cross-sectional area]
   Unit: S/m or S/cm

Cell Constant = l/A (property of the cell, not the solution)

Molar Conductance (Λm):
   Λm = κ × 1000/M    [M = molarity, κ in S/cm, gives Λm in S·cm²/mol]
   OR Λm = κ/c          [c = concentration in mol/m³, gives Λm in S·m²/mol]

Equivalent Conductance (Λeq):
   Λeq = Λm / n-factor (less common in modern JEE but useful to know)

Variation with concentration:
→ Strong electrolyte: Λm increases gradually as concentration decreases (Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation)
   Λm = Λm° - b√c [Kohlrausch's empirical equation]
→ Weak electrolyte: Λm increases sharply at very low concentration (degree of ionization increases)

Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration of Ions:
   Λm° = λ°+ + λ°- (for 1:1 electrolyte)
   [At infinite dilution, each ion migrates independently]
   
   General: Λm° = ν+ λ°+ + ν- λ°-
   [ν+ and ν- = stoichiometric coefficients of cation and anion]

JEE APPLICATION: Calculate Λm° for weak acid using Kohlrausch's law.
   Λm°(CH3COOH) = Λm°(HCl) + Λm°(CH3COONa) - Λm°(NaCl)
   [Cannot measure directly by extrapolation for weak acids]

Degree of dissociation of weak electrolyte:
   α = Λm / Λm°
   [At concentration c, fraction that has dissociated]

JEE TRAP: Kohlrausch's law allows calculation of Λm° for WEAK electrolytes indirectly, since they can't be extrapolated on the Λm vs √c graph (it's not linear for weak electrolytes).

SECTION 5: ELECTROCHEMICAL CELLS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Galvanic (Voltaic) Cell: Converts chemical energy → electrical energy (spontaneous reaction).
Electrolytic Cell: Converts electrical energy → chemical energy (non-spontaneous reaction forced by external voltage).

Galvanic Cell — Example: Daniell Cell (Zn-Cu cell):
   Zn(s) | ZnSO4(aq) || CuSO4(aq) | Cu(s)
   Anode (negative): Zn → Zn2+ + 2e- (oxidation, left side)
   Cathode (positive): Cu2+ + 2e- → Cu (reduction, right side)
   Net: Zn + Cu2+ → Zn2+ + Cu

Cell Notation (by convention):
   Anode || Cathode (single vertical line = electrode-solution interface, double || = salt bridge)
   Oxidation at left (anode), Reduction at right (cathode).

Salt Bridge:
→ Contains concentrated electrolyte (like KNO3 or KCl in agar-agar gel).
→ Maintains electrical neutrality by allowing ion migration.
→ Completes the circuit.

SECTION 6: ELECTRODE POTENTIALS AND EMF
----------------------------------------------------------------

Electrode Potential: Potential difference between electrode and electrolyte.
Standard Electrode Potential (E°): measured at 25°C, 1 M concentration, 1 atm.

Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE): E° = 0.00 V (reference electrode)
   H+(1M) + e- → ½H2(g, 1atm)  E° = 0.00 V

Cell EMF:
   E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode = E°reduction(right) - E°reduction(left)
   OR E°cell = E°cathode + E°anode (if E°anode given as oxidation potential)

For spontaneous cell: E°cell must be positive (ΔG < 0).

Standard Reduction Potentials (must know for JEE):
   F2/F-: +2.87 V (strongest oxidizer)
   MnO4-/Mn2+: +1.51 V
   Cr2O7²-/Cr3+: +1.33 V
   Cl2/Cl-: +1.36 V
   O2/H2O: +1.23 V
   Fe3+/Fe2+: +0.77 V
   I2/I-: +0.54 V
   Cu2+/Cu: +0.34 V
   H+/H2: 0.00 V (SHE)
   Pb2+/Pb: -0.13 V
   Fe2+/Fe: -0.44 V
   Zn2+/Zn: -0.76 V
   Al3+/Al: -1.66 V
   Mg2+/Mg: -2.37 V
   Na+/Na: -2.71 V
   Li+/Li: -3.04 V (strongest reducer)

Higher reduction potential = better oxidizing agent.
Lower reduction potential (more negative) = better reducing agent.

JEE TRAP: A higher standard reduction potential means the species is MORE easily reduced (better oxidizing agent). A metal with very negative E° is easily oxidized (good reducing agent).

SECTION 7: NERNST EQUATION
----------------------------------------------------------------

At non-standard conditions:
   E = E° - (RT/nF) ln Q
   E = E° - (0.0592/n) log Q    [at 25°C = 298 K]
   
   Where: n = moles of electrons transferred, Q = reaction quotient

At equilibrium: E = 0, Q = K
   E° = (0.0592/n) log K    [at 25°C]
   [This links electrochemistry to equilibrium constant]

JEE APPLICATIONS:
→ Concentration cell (same electrode material, different concentrations):
   E = (0.0592/n) log [C2/C1]  [where C2 > C1 at cathode]

→ Cell potential changes with concentration.
→ More dilute the electrolyte at cathode, lower the potential.

Relationship: ΔG = -nFE
   ΔG° = -nFE°
   n = number of electrons, F = Faraday = 96500 C/mol

If E > 0 → ΔG < 0 → spontaneous.
If E = 0 → ΔG = 0 → equilibrium.
If E < 0 → ΔG > 0 → non-spontaneous.

SECTION 8: ELECTROLYSIS AND FARADAY'S LAWS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Electrolytic Cell: External battery forces non-spontaneous reaction.
Anode (positive terminal): Oxidation occurs here.
Cathode (negative terminal): Reduction occurs here.

JEE TRAP: In electrolytic cells, anode is positive (opposite to galvanic cell where anode is negative).

Faraday's First Law:
   Mass deposited ∝ quantity of charge (coulombs)
   w = (E/F) × Q = (M/nF) × I × t
   [E = equivalent mass, M = molar mass, n = n-factor, I = current in amperes, t = time in seconds]

Faraday's Second Law:
   When same charge passes through different electrolytes, masses deposited are proportional to their equivalent masses.

Faraday's Constant (F) = 96500 C/mol = charge on 1 mole of electrons.

JEE CALCULATIONS:
   Moles of electrons = I × t / F = Q/F
   Moles of substance deposited = (I × t) / (n × F)
   Mass deposited = molar mass × moles = (M × I × t) / (n × F)

ELECTROLYSIS OF WATER: 2H2O → 2H2 + O2
→ Cathode: 2H2O + 2e- → H2 + 2OH- (or 2H+ + 2e- → H2 in acid)
→ Anode: 2H2O → O2 + 4H+ + 4e-
→ Volume of H2 produced is TWICE the volume of O2 (ratio 2:1)

ELECTROLYSIS OF NaCl(aq) — Chlor-alkali process:
→ Cathode: 2H2O + 2e- → H2 + 2OH-
→ Anode: 2Cl- → Cl2 + 2e-
→ Products: H2, Cl2, NaOH (sodium hydroxide)
→ Important industrial process.

SECTION 9: BATTERIES AND FUEL CELLS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Primary Cells (non-rechargeable):

Dry Cell (Leclanche Cell):
→ Anode: Zn (Zinc can — the outer casing)
→ Cathode: Graphite rod in MnO2 + NH4Cl paste
→ Reactions: Zn → Zn2+ + 2e- (anode)
              MnO2 + NH4+ + e- → MnO(OH) + NH3 (cathode)
→ EMF ≈ 1.5 V

Mercury Cell:
→ Anode: Zn-Hg amalgam, Cathode: HgO in KOH paste
→ Constant voltage throughout, used in watches, hearing aids.

Secondary Cells (rechargeable):

Lead Storage Battery (lead accumulator):
→ Anode: Pb (lead), Cathode: PbO2, Electrolyte: H2SO4 (38%)
→ Discharge (use): Pb + SO4²- → PbSO4 + 2e- (anode)
                    PbO2 + SO4²- + 4H+ + 2e- → PbSO4 + 2H2O (cathode)
→ Charge (recharging): reactions reversed
→ During discharge: H2SO4 is consumed → density of H2SO4 decreases → check battery health by density measurement
→ EMF ≈ 2.0 V per cell, 6 cells = 12 V car battery

Nickel-Cadmium Cell: Cd/Cd(OH)2 // NiOOH/Ni(OH)2

Fuel Cells (galvanic cells using continuous fuel supply):
→ Hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell: H2 + O2 → H2O, produces electricity without combustion.
→ High efficiency, used in spacecraft, zero emission.

SECTION 10: JEE-SPECIFIC PATTERNS
----------------------------------------------------------------

HIGH FREQUENCY QUESTION TYPES:
1. Calculate E°cell from standard reduction potentials
2. Apply Nernst equation to find cell potential at non-standard conditions
3. Find mass deposited in electrolysis using Faraday's law
4. Assign oxidation numbers and balance redox equations
5. Calculate ΔG from cell potential or vice versa
6. Identify stronger oxidizing/reducing agent from reduction potentials
7. Kohlrausch's law calculations for molar conductance
8. Identify anode/cathode in galvanic and electrolytic cells

CRITICAL TRAPS:
→ Galvanic cell: anode = negative, cathode = positive.
   Electrolytic cell: anode = positive (connected to + terminal of battery), cathode = negative.
→ E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode (both as reduction potentials). NEVER add them directly.
→ When n is determined for Faraday's law: n = change in oxidation state × number of atoms.
→ Lead battery: during USE, PbSO4 forms at BOTH electrodes. H2SO4 is consumed.
→ Kohlrausch's law applies at INFINITE dilution (limiting molar conductance).
→ Conductance of solution increases with temperature (metallic conduction DECREASES with T).
→ Concentration cell: same electrodes, different concentrations. Electrode with higher concentration is cathode.
