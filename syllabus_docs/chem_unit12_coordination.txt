SECTION 1: BASIC CONCEPTS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Coordination Compound:
→ Compound containing a central metal atom/ion bonded to a group of surrounding molecules or ions called ligands.
→ Key distinction: coordination compounds retain their identity in solution (unlike double salts like alums which dissociate completely).
→ Example: [Co(NH3)6]Cl3 → in solution gives [Co(NH3)6]3+ and 3Cl-, NOT free Co3+ and NH3.

Werner's Theory:
→ Central metal ion has TWO types of valency:
   1. Primary valency (ionizable): satisfied by counter ions (like Cl- outside brackets). Equal to oxidation state.
   2. Secondary valency (non-ionizable): satisfied by ligands. Equal to coordination number.
→ Secondary valency is directional → determines geometry of complex.

Key Terms:
Central metal atom/ion: atom bonded to ligands (usually transition metal).
Coordination sphere: central metal + all ligands enclosed in square brackets [ ].
Counter ion: ions outside coordination sphere that balance overall charge.
Coordination number (CN): total number of sigma bonds made by metal with ligands (total donor atoms).

Effective Atomic Number (EAN): NOT required in JEE 2026 syllabus — skip detailed calculations.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: LIGANDS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Ligand: molecule or ion that donates electron pair(s) to central metal. Always a Lewis base.

Classification by number of donor atoms (denticity):
→ Monodentate: donates 1 electron pair. (F-, Cl-, Br-, I-, NH3, H2O, OH-, CN-, CO, NO2-)
→ Bidentate: donates 2 electron pairs. (en = ethylenediamine H2N-CH2-CH2-NH2, C2O4²- = oxalate, acac)
→ Tridentate: 3 donor atoms. (diethylenetriamine = dien)
→ Tetradentate: 4 donor atoms. (triethylenetetramine = trien)
→ Hexadentate: 6 donor atoms. (EDTA = ethylenediaminetetraacetate)

Chelate ligands: bi- or polydentate ligands that form rings with central metal.
→ Chelate complexes are MORE STABLE than analogous complexes with monodentate ligands (chelate effect = increased entropy on formation).
→ EDTA is a hexadentate chelating agent — used to treat heavy metal poisoning.

Ambidentate ligand: monodentate ligand that can bind through two different atoms.
→ SCN- can bind through S (thiocyanato-S or thiocyanate) or through N (thiocyanato-N or isothiocyanate).
→ NO2- can bind through N (nitro, -NO2) or through O (nitrito, -ONO).

Bridging ligand: connects two or more metal centers. Denoted with μ- prefix.
Example: μ-Cl means Cl is bridging two metals.

Important Ligands and Their Names:
F-: fluoro       |  Cl-: chloro    |  Br-: bromo     |  I-: iodo
OH-: hydroxo     |  O²-: oxo       |  CN-: cyano      |  SCN-: thiocyanato
NO2- (N): nitro  |  NO2- (O): nitrito |  CO: carbonyl  |  NO: nitrosyl
NH3: ammine      |  H2O: aqua      |  en: ethylenediamine  |  ox²-: oxalato
PPh3: triphenylphosphine

SECTION 3: IUPAC NOMENCLATURE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Order of naming:
1. Cation first, then anion (like any other ionic compound).
2. Within coordination sphere: ligands first (alphabetically), then metal name + oxidation state.
3. Cationic complex: metal name as is (cobalt, chromium, iron).
4. Anionic complex: metal name gets -ate suffix (cobaltate, chromate, ferrate, cuprate, argentate, aurate).
   Note special names: Fe → ferrate, Cu → cuprate, Ag → argentate, Au → aurate, Pb → plumbate, Sn → stannate.

Naming ligands:
→ Neutral ligands: name as molecule (aqua, ammine, carbonyl, nitrosyl) — exceptions from common names.
→ Anionic ligands: replace -ide with -o (chloro, bromo, hydroxo, oxo, cyano), -ate with -ato (sulfato, oxalato, acetato).
→ Cationic ligands: add -ium (rare in JEE).

Prefix for number of ligands:
→ Simple: di, tri, tetra, penta, hexa
→ Complex ligand names: bis, tris, tetrakis, pentakis, hexakis (to avoid confusion with di/tri in ligand name)
   Example: bis(ethylenediamine) not diethylenediamine.

IUPAC NAMING EXAMPLES:
[Co(NH3)6]Cl3: hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride
[Pt(Cl)2(NH3)2]: diamminedichloridoplatinum(II)
K4[Fe(CN)6]: potassium hexacyanoferrate(II) [ferrocyanide]
K3[Fe(CN)6]: potassium hexacyanoferrate(III) [ferricyanide]
[CrCl3(en)]: trichlorido(ethylenediamine)chromium(III) — wait, en is bidentate → different formula
[Co(en)3]3+: tris(ethylenediamine)cobalt(III) ion
[Ni(CO)4]: tetracarbonylnickel(0)
Na2[PtCl4]: sodium tetrachloridoplatinate(II)
[Fe(CO)5]: pentacarbonyliron(0)

JEE TRAP: In anionic complexes — metal gets -ate suffix. So iron becomes ferrate, copper becomes cuprate.
In cationic or neutral complexes — metal name is unchanged.

SECTION 4: ISOMERISM IN COORDINATION COMPOUNDS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Two main types: Structural Isomerism and Stereoisomerism.

STRUCTURAL ISOMERISM:

1. Ionization Isomerism: Same formula, different ions inside/outside coordination sphere.
   [Co(NH3)5Br]SO4 and [Co(NH3)5SO4]Br
   (First gives SO4²- in solution, second gives Br- in solution — test with AgNO3 vs BaCl2)

2. Linkage Isomerism: Ambidentate ligand binds through different atoms.
   [Co(NH3)5(NO2)]Cl2 (nitro, N-bonded) and [Co(NH3)5(ONO)]Cl2 (nitrito, O-bonded)

3. Coordination Isomerism: Both cation and anion are complex ions — exchange of ligands.
   [Co(en)3][Cr(ox)3] and [Cr(en)3][Co(ox)3]

4. Solvate (Hydrate) Isomerism: Different number of water molecules in coordination sphere vs outside.
   [Cr(H2O)6]Cl3 (violet), [CrCl(H2O)5]Cl2·H2O (blue-green), [CrCl2(H2O)4]Cl·2H2O (dark green)

STEREOISOMERISM:

1. Geometrical Isomerism (cis-trans):
   For square planar [MA2B2]: cis has same ligands adjacent, trans has same ligands opposite.
   Example: cis-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2] = cisplatin (anticancer drug), trans-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2] = transplatin (inactive).
   For octahedral [MA4B2]: cis has B's at 90°, trans has B's at 180°.
   [MA3B3]: fac (facial — 3 identical ligands on one face) and mer (meridional — 3 on plane).

2. Optical Isomerism (mirror image non-superimposable):
   [Co(en)3]3+: exists as d and l (Δ and Λ) forms — chiral complex.
   cis-[Co(en)2Cl2]+: optically active (chiral).
   trans-[Co(en)2Cl2]+: has plane of symmetry, NOT optically active.

JEE TRAP: Square planar complexes don't show optical isomerism (they have a plane of symmetry — the molecular plane). Only tetrahedral and octahedral complexes can be optically active.

SECTION 5: BONDING — VALENCE BOND THEORY (VBT)
----------------------------------------------------------------

VBT: Metal uses hybridized orbitals to accept electron pairs from ligands.

HYBRIDIZATION AND GEOMETRY:
CN=2: sp (linear)
CN=4: sp³ (tetrahedral) or dsp² (square planar)
CN=6: d²sp³ (inner orbital, strong field ligands) or sp³d² (outer orbital, weak field ligands)

Strong field ligands (cause large splitting, paired electrons): CO, CN-, NO2-, en, NH3
Weak field ligands (cause small splitting, more unpaired electrons): F-, Cl-, Br-, I-, H2O, OH-

Spectrochemical Series (increasing field strength):
I- < Br- < Cl- < F- < OH- < H2O < NH3 < en < NO2- < CN- < CO

INNER ORBITAL COMPLEX (Low spin, strong field):
→ d²sp³ hybridization (uses inner 3d orbitals).
→ Ligands force electron pairing, fewer unpaired electrons.
→ Fewer or zero unpaired electrons → weakly paramagnetic or diamagnetic.

OUTER ORBITAL COMPLEX (High spin, weak field):
→ sp³d² hybridization (uses outer 4d orbitals).
→ Electrons remain unpaired as much as possible.
→ More unpaired electrons → more paramagnetic.

JEE EXAMPLES:
[Fe(CN)6]⁴-: CN- is strong field → d²sp³ (inner), 0 unpaired electrons (diamagnetic), Fe2+: 3d⁶
[Fe(H2O)6]²+: H2O is weak field → sp³d², 4 unpaired electrons (paramagnetic), Fe2+: 3d⁶

[Co(NH3)6]³+: NH3 is strong field → d²sp³, 0 unpaired (diamagnetic), Co3+: 3d⁶

SECTION 6: CRYSTAL FIELD THEORY (CFT) — BASICS
----------------------------------------------------------------

CFT treats ligands as point charges. d-orbitals split due to electrostatic repulsion from ligands.

In OCTAHEDRAL field:
→ d-orbitals split into:
   t2g (lower energy): dxy, dxz, dyz (3 orbitals)
   eg (higher energy): dx²-y², dz² (2 orbitals)
→ Crystal field splitting energy (Δo or 10Dq): energy gap between t2g and eg.

In TETRAHEDRAL field:
→ Splitting is reversed: e (lower) and t2 (higher)
→ Δt = 4/9 × Δo (much smaller than octahedral)

Colour from CFT:
→ d-electrons absorb wavelength corresponding to Δ (jump from t2g to eg).
→ Transmitted/reflected color is complementary color of absorbed.
→ Strong field ligands → large Δ → absorb shorter wavelengths (UV/blue end).
→ Weak field ligands → small Δ → absorb longer wavelengths (red/yellow end).

JEE TRAP: The colour of coordination complexes is due to d-d transition (energy gap Δ). Changing ligand changes Δ → changes colour. Same metal, different ligands → different colours.

Pairing energy (P): energy required to pair two electrons in same orbital.
→ If Δ > P: electrons pair in t2g before filling eg → LOW SPIN (strong field ligands)
→ If Δ < P: electrons fill all orbitals singly before pairing → HIGH SPIN (weak field ligands)

Magnetic Properties (from CFT):
Number of unpaired electrons → magnetic moment μ = √[n(n+2)] BM

SECTION 7: IMPORTANCE OF COORDINATION COMPOUNDS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Biological Importance:
→ Haemoglobin: Fe2+ coordinated to porphyrin ring (heme) and protein globin. Carries O2.
→ Chlorophyll: Mg2+ coordinated to porphyrin ring. Essential for photosynthesis.
→ Vitamin B12 (cyanocobalamin): Co3+ coordination compound.
→ Carbonic anhydrase: Zn2+ enzyme that hydrates CO2 in blood.

Analytical Chemistry:
→ EDTA titrations for Ca2+ and Mg2+ determination (water hardness).
→ [Ni(dmg)2] (nickel dimethylglyoxime): red precipitate — test for Ni2+.
→ [Fe(SCN)n]^(3-n): blood red color — test for Fe3+.
→ Blue coloration: [Cu(NH3)4]2+ (tetraamminecopper — deep blue).

Metallurgy:
→ Extraction of gold: 4Au + 8NaCN + 2H2O + O2 → 4Na[Au(CN)2] + 4NaOH (leaching)
→ Extraction of silver: similar cyanide process.
→ Purification of Ni: Mond process using Ni(CO)4.

Medicine:
→ cisplatin [Pt(NH3)2Cl2]: anticancer drug (binds to DNA, stops cell division).
→ EDTA: chelation therapy for heavy metal poisoning (Pb, Hg).
→ Desferrioxamine: chelating agent for iron overload.

JEE TRAP: In haemoglobin, Fe is in +2 state. In haematin, Fe is in +3 state (hemoglobin oxidized — cannot carry O2, causes methemoglobinemia).

SECTION 8: JEE-SPECIFIC PATTERNS
----------------------------------------------------------------

HIGH FREQUENCY QUESTION TYPES:
1. Write IUPAC name of given coordination compound
2. Write formula from given IUPAC name
3. Determine geometry and hybridization of complex ion
4. Find number of unpaired electrons and magnetic moment
5. Identify type of isomerism in given pair of compounds
6. Calculate oxidation state of metal in complex
7. Identify whether complex is inner or outer orbital using field strength

CRITICAL TRAPS:
→ Coordination number ≠ number of ligands. Bidentate ligands increase CN by 2 each.
   [Co(en)3]3+: 3 ligands but CN = 6 (each en donates 2 electron pairs).
→ Counter ions are OUTSIDE the bracket. They are separated in solution.
   [CoBr(NH3)5]SO4: gives [CoBr(NH3)5]2+ and SO4²- in solution. Does NOT give Co3+ separately.
→ In anionic complex name: iron → ferrate, copper → cuprate, silver → argentate, gold → aurate, lead → plumbate, tin → stannate.
→ Optical isomerism: trans-octahedral MA4B2 and MA4BC are NOT optically active. cis-MA4B2 can be. en complexes often are.
→ Cisplatin (cis-) is active anticancer. Transplatin (trans-) is not.
→ Δt = 4/9 Δo: tetrahedral complexes always have smaller splitting → mostly high spin.
