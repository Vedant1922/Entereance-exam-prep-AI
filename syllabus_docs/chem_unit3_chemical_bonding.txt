SECTION 1: IONIC BONDING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Ionic bond forms by complete transfer of electron(s) from metal to non-metal.
Driving force: Both atoms achieve noble gas configuration (octet).

Formation conditions (favorable when):
→ Low ionization enthalpy of metal (easily loses electrons)
→ High electron gain enthalpy of non-metal (easily gains electrons)
→ High lattice enthalpy (strong electrostatic attraction in crystal)

Lattice Enthalpy:
→ Energy released when 1 mole of ionic crystal is formed from gaseous ions.
→ Higher lattice enthalpy = more stable ionic compound.
→ Lattice enthalpy ∝ (charge of ions) / (distance between ions)
→ MgO has higher lattice enthalpy than NaCl because Mg2+ and O2- have higher charges than Na+ and Cl-.

Fajan's Rules — Determines covalent character in ionic bonds:
Covalent character increases when:
1. Cation is small with high charge (high charge density / polarizing power)
2. Anion is large (easily polarizable / high polarizability)

JEE APPLICATION: 
→ AgCl has more covalent character than NaCl (Ag+ is more polarizing than Na+)
→ AlCl3 has significant covalent character (Al3+ is small with high charge)
→ LiI has more covalent character than LiF (I- is larger, more polarizable)

SECTION 2: COVALENT BONDING AND LEWIS STRUCTURES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Covalent bond = sharing of electron pair(s) between atoms.
Single bond: 1 shared pair. Double bond: 2 shared pairs. Triple bond: 3 shared pairs.

Lewis Structure Rules:
1. Count total valence electrons.
2. Connect atoms with single bonds (each uses 2 electrons).
3. Complete octets of outer atoms first, then central atom.
4. If central atom lacks octet, form multiple bonds.
5. Minimize formal charges.

Formal Charge = Valence electrons - Lone pair electrons - (1/2 × Bonding electrons)

Octet rule exceptions (know all of these for JEE):
→ Incomplete octet: BeCl2 (Be has 4 electrons), BF3 (B has 6 electrons)
→ Expanded octet: PCl5 (P has 10 electrons), SF6 (S has 12 electrons) — only Period 3+ elements
→ Odd electron molecules: NO, NO2 (have unpaired electron — paramagnetic)

Resonance:
→ When a molecule cannot be represented by a single Lewis structure.
→ Actual structure is hybrid of all resonance structures.
→ Resonance lowers energy (more stable than any individual structure).
→ All resonance structures have same arrangement of atoms, only electron distribution differs.
→ Bond lengths in resonance hybrid are intermediate between single and double bond.

Examples: O3 (ozone), CO3²⁻, NO3⁻, C6H6 (benzene)
→ In benzene, all C-C bonds are equal length (1.40 Å — between single 1.54 and double 1.34)

Dipole Moment (μ):
   μ = q × d (charge × distance)
   Unit: Debye (D) = 3.336 × 10^-30 C·m

→ Measures polarity of bond or molecule.
→ For molecule: vector sum of all bond dipoles.
→ Symmetric molecules have μ = 0 even if individual bonds are polar.

JEE EXAMPLES:
→ CO2: μ = 0 (linear, symmetric, bond dipoles cancel)
→ H2O: μ ≠ 0 (bent shape, bond dipoles don't cancel)
→ BF3: μ = 0 (trigonal planar, symmetric)
→ NF3: μ ≠ 0 (pyramidal, lone pair contributes)
→ CHCl3: μ ≠ 0 (asymmetric)
→ CCl4: μ = 0 (tetrahedral, symmetric)

JEE TRAP: CCl4 has polar C-Cl bonds but μ = 0 due to symmetry. CHCl3 has μ ≠ 0 because symmetry is broken.

Electronegativity:
→ Ability of an atom in a bond to attract shared electron pair toward itself.
→ Pauling scale: F is highest (4.0), then O (3.5), N (3.0), Cl (3.0)
→ Increases across period (left to right), decreases down group.
→ Bond polarity increases with electronegativity difference.

SECTION 3: VSEPR THEORY AND MOLECULAR SHAPES
----------------------------------------------------------------

VSEPR = Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion Theory
Principle: Electron pairs (both bonding and lone pairs) arrange themselves to minimize repulsion.

Repulsion order: Lone pair-Lone pair > Lone pair-Bonding pair > Bonding pair-Bonding pair
→ Lone pairs take up more space → compress bond angles.

VSEPR SHAPES — MASTER TABLE:

Bonding Pairs | Lone Pairs | Geometry | Shape | Bond Angle | Example
2 | 0 | Linear | Linear | 180° | CO2, BeCl2
3 | 0 | Trigonal Planar | Trigonal Planar | 120° | BF3, SO3
2 | 1 | Trigonal Planar | Bent/V-shaped | <120° | SO2
4 | 0 | Tetrahedral | Tetrahedral | 109.5° | CH4, CCl4
3 | 1 | Tetrahedral | Trigonal Pyramidal | <109.5° | NH3 (107°)
2 | 2 | Tetrahedral | Bent/V-shaped | <109.5° | H2O (104.5°)
5 | 0 | Trigonal Bipyramidal | Trigonal Bipyramidal | 90°,120° | PCl5
4 | 1 | Trigonal Bipyramidal | See-saw | — | SF4
3 | 2 | Trigonal Bipyramidal | T-shaped | — | ClF3
2 | 3 | Trigonal Bipyramidal | Linear | 180° | XeF2, I3-
6 | 0 | Octahedral | Octahedral | 90° | SF6
5 | 1 | Octahedral | Square Pyramidal | — | ClF5
4 | 2 | Octahedral | Square Planar | 90° | XeF4, [PtCl4]²-

Bond Angle Trend:
→ CH4 (109.5°) > NH3 (107°) > H2O (104.5°)
→ Reason: Each lone pair reduces bond angle by ~2-2.5°

JEE TRAP: Geometry refers to ALL electron pairs. Shape refers only to atom positions.
Example: NH3 geometry = tetrahedral (4 pairs), shape = trigonal pyramidal (3 bonds + 1 lone pair).

SECTION 4: HYBRIDIZATION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Hybridization = mixing of atomic orbitals of similar energy to form new equivalent hybrid orbitals.

sp Hybridization:
→ 1 s + 1 p → 2 sp orbitals
→ Linear geometry, 180° bond angle
→ Two unhybridized p orbitals available for π bonding
→ Examples: BeCl2, C2H2 (acetylene), CO2, HCN

sp² Hybridization:
→ 1 s + 2 p → 3 sp² orbitals
→ Trigonal planar geometry, 120° bond angle
→ One unhybridized p orbital available for π bonding
→ Examples: BF3, C2H4 (ethylene), SO3, benzene (C6H6)

sp³ Hybridization:
→ 1 s + 3 p → 4 sp³ orbitals
→ Tetrahedral geometry, 109.5° bond angle
→ No unhybridized p orbitals
→ Examples: CH4, NH3, H2O, CCl4

sp³d Hybridization:
→ 5 hybrid orbitals
→ Trigonal bipyramidal geometry
→ Examples: PCl5, SF4, ClF3, XeF2

sp³d² Hybridization:
→ 6 hybrid orbitals
→ Octahedral geometry
→ Examples: SF6, XeF4, [Co(NH3)6]3+

JEE FORMULA to find hybridization:
   H = (1/2) × [V + M - C + A]
   V = valence electrons of central atom
   M = monovalent atoms attached
   C = charge if cation (subtract)
   A = charge if anion (add)
   H=2 → sp, H=3 → sp², H=4 → sp³, H=5 → sp³d, H=6 → sp³d²

Examples:
→ CH4: H = (4+4)/2 = 4 → sp³
→ NH3: H = (5+3)/2 = 4 → sp³ (lone pair occupies one sp³ orbital)
→ H2O: H = (6+2)/2 = 4 → sp³ (two lone pairs)
→ PCl5: H = (5+5)/2 = 5 → sp³d
→ SF6: H = (6+6)/2 = 6 → sp³d²
→ NO3⁻: H = (5+0+1)/2 = 3 → sp² (add 1 for negative charge)

JEE TRAP: Hybridization of carbon in C2H2 is sp even though it has 3 bonds. Each carbon makes 1 sigma (sp-sp) + 2 pi bonds (p-p). Count sigma bonds around each carbon: 2 → sp.

SECTION 5: MOLECULAR ORBITAL THEORY (MOT)
----------------------------------------------------------------

MOT treats bonding from a quantum mechanical perspective.
Linear Combination of Atomic Orbitals (LCAO): Atomic orbitals combine to form molecular orbitals.

Two AOs combine to form:
→ 1 Bonding MO (lower energy, electron density between nuclei)
→ 1 Antibonding MO* (higher energy, node between nuclei, denoted with asterisk)

Types of MO:
→ σ (sigma): formed by head-on overlap (along internuclear axis)
→ π (pi): formed by lateral/sideways overlap (perpendicular to axis)
→ σ* and π* are corresponding antibonding MOs

ENERGY ORDER OF MOs (for N2, O2, F2 and similar diatomics):
For O2, F2, Ne2: σ1s < σ*1s < σ2s < σ*2s < σ2p < π2p = π2p < π*2p = π*2p < σ*2p
For Li2 to N2: σ1s < σ*1s < σ2s < σ*2s < π2p = π2p < σ2p < π*2p = π*2p < σ*2p
[Note: for Li2 to N2, π2p fills before σ2p]

Bond Order = (Bonding electrons - Antibonding electrons) / 2
→ Higher bond order = stronger bond = shorter bond length = higher bond energy
→ Bond order = 0 → molecule doesn't exist (e.g., He2)
→ Fractional bond order is possible (e.g., O2⁻ has BO = 1.5)

CRITICAL EXAMPLES FOR JEE:

H2: σ1s² → Bond order = (2-0)/2 = 1, Diamagnetic
He2: σ1s²σ*1s² → Bond order = (2-2)/2 = 0, Doesn't exist

N2: ...σ2p² → BO = 3, Diamagnetic (all electrons paired)
O2: ...π*2p¹π*2p¹ → BO = 2, PARAMAGNETIC (2 unpaired electrons in degenerate π* MOs)
F2: ...π*2p²π*2p² → BO = 1, Diamagnetic

JEE TRAP: O2 is PARAMAGNETIC despite Lewis structure showing no unpaired electrons. This is the most important result of MOT. Lewis structure fails here, MOT succeeds.

Magnetic Properties:
→ Paramagnetic: has unpaired electrons in MOs (attracted to magnetic field)
→ Diamagnetic: all electrons paired (weakly repelled by magnetic field)

Bond Order comparison:
→ O2 > O2⁺ > O2⁻ > O2²⁻
   BO:  2       2.5      1.5       1
→ N2 > N2⁺: BO 3 > 2.5

JEE TRAP: Removing electron from O2 to form O2⁺ removes from antibonding → bond ORDER INCREASES.
Removing electron from N2 to form N2⁺ removes from bonding → bond order DECREASES.

SECTION 6: HYDROGEN BONDING
----------------------------------------------------------------

Hydrogen bond forms when H is bonded to a highly electronegative atom (F, O, N) AND is near another electronegative atom.

Intermolecular H-bond: between different molecules (e.g., H2O, HF, NH3, alcohols)
Intramolecular H-bond: within same molecule (e.g., o-nitrophenol, salicylaldehyde)

Strength: F-H···F > O-H···O > N-H···N

Effects of H-bonding:
→ Increases boiling point significantly (extra energy needed to break H-bonds)
→ H2O has anomalously high BP compared to H2S, H2Se, H2Te (all of which have no strong H-bonding)
→ HF has higher BP than HCl, HBr, HI (same reason)
→ NH3 has higher BP than PH3, AsH3 (same reason)

JEE TRAP: Why is HF bp lower than H2O bp despite F being more electronegative?
Because H2O forms 4 H-bonds per molecule (more extensive network), HF forms 2.

o-Nitrophenol has lower BP than p-nitrophenol:
→ o-nitrophenol has intramolecular H-bonding (within molecule) → cannot form intermolecular H-bonds → lower BP
→ p-nitrophenol has intermolecular H-bonding → higher BP

Ice has lower density than water:
→ Hexagonal open cage structure due to H-bonding → less dense than liquid water.
