SECTION 1: CONCENTRATION EXPRESSIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Molarity (M):
   M = moles of solute / volume of solution in litres
   Unit: mol/L or M
   → Depends on temperature (volume changes with T)
   → Most commonly used in reactions and titrations

Molality (m):
   m = moles of solute / mass of solvent in kg
   Unit: mol/kg
   → Does NOT depend on temperature (mass doesn't change with T)
   → Used in colligative property calculations

Mole Fraction (χ):
   χA = nA / (nA + nB)
   Sum of all mole fractions = 1
   χA + χB = 1 (for binary solution)

Percentage by mass (w/w):
   % = (mass of solute / mass of solution) × 100

Percentage by volume (v/v):
   % = (volume of solute / volume of solution) × 100

Parts per million (ppm):
   ppm = (mass of component / total mass of solution) × 10^6
   Used for very dilute solutions (like pollutants in water)

CONVERSIONS (commonly asked in JEE):
Molarity to Molality:
   m = (M × 1000) / (1000 × d - M × M_solute)
   [d = density in g/mL, M_solute = molar mass of solute]

Molarity to Mole Fraction:
   χsolute = M / (M + 1000d/M_solvent - M × M_solute/M_solvent)

JEE TRAP: Molarity uses SOLUTION volume. Molality uses SOLVENT mass. Students often mix these up.

SECTION 2: VAPOUR PRESSURE AND RAOULT'S LAW
----------------------------------------------------------------

Vapour Pressure: Pressure exerted by vapour in equilibrium with its liquid at given temperature.
→ Increases with temperature.
→ Liquid boils when vapour pressure = external pressure.

Raoult's Law (for ideal solutions):
   PA = χA × PA°
   [Partial pressure of A = mole fraction of A × vapour pressure of pure A]

For binary solution (A = solvent, B = solute):
   Ptotal = PA + PB = χA·PA° + χB·PB°

Ideal Solutions:
→ Follow Raoult's law at all concentrations.
→ ΔHmix = 0, ΔVmix = 0
→ Intermolecular forces between A-A, B-B, and A-B are identical.
→ Examples: benzene + toluene, hexane + heptane, CCl4 + SiCl4

Non-Ideal Solutions:
Positive deviation (A-B interactions weaker than A-A and B-B):
→ P > Raoult's law prediction (higher vapour pressure)
→ ΔHmix > 0 (endothermic mixing), ΔVmix > 0
→ Examples: ethanol + water, acetone + CS2, acetone + benzene
→ May show minimum boiling azeotrope (e.g., ethanol-water 95.6% ethanol, bp 78.1°C)

Negative deviation (A-B interactions stronger than A-A and B-B):
→ P < Raoult's law prediction (lower vapour pressure)
→ ΔHmix < 0 (exothermic mixing), ΔVmix < 0
→ Examples: acetone + chloroform, HCl + water, HNO3 + water
→ May show maximum boiling azeotrope (e.g., HCl-water 20.2% HCl, bp 108.6°C)

Henry's Law (for dissolved gases):
   p = KH × χ
   [Partial pressure of gas = Henry's constant × mole fraction of gas in solution]
→ Higher temperature → lower solubility of gas (KH increases)
→ Higher pressure → higher solubility of gas (scuba diving, carbonated drinks)

JEE TRAP: Raoult's law applies to the SOLVENT in dilute solutions. Henry's law applies to the SOLUTE (gas dissolved in liquid).

SECTION 3: COLLIGATIVE PROPERTIES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Colligative properties depend ONLY on the NUMBER of solute particles, not their nature/identity.
Four colligative properties in JEE:

1. RELATIVE LOWERING OF VAPOUR PRESSURE (RLVP):

   (PA° - PA) / PA° = χB (mole fraction of solute)

   For dilute solutions: (PA° - PA) / PA° ≈ nB/nA ≈ wB·MA / (wA·MB)

   JEE: This is used to find molecular mass of solute.

2. ELEVATION OF BOILING POINT (ΔTb):
   
   Solution boils at higher temperature than pure solvent (because vapour pressure is lowered).
   
   ΔTb = Tb(solution) - Tb(solvent) = Kb × m
   
   Kb = Ebullioscopic constant (molal elevation constant) — property of solvent only.
   Kb(water) = 0.512 K·kg/mol
   
   For finding molar mass: M_solute = (Kb × w_B × 1000) / (ΔTb × w_A)
   [wA = mass of solvent in g, wB = mass of solute in g]

3. DEPRESSION OF FREEZING POINT (ΔTf):
   
   Solution freezes at lower temperature than pure solvent.
   
   ΔTf = Tf(solvent) - Tf(solution) = Kf × m
   
   Kf = Cryoscopic constant (molal depression constant) — property of solvent only.
   Kf(water) = 1.86 K·kg/mol (MUST MEMORIZE)
   Kf(benzene) = 5.12 K·kg/mol
   
   For finding molar mass: M_solute = (Kf × w_B × 1000) / (ΔTf × w_A)
   
   JEE APPLICATION: Antifreeze in car radiators (ethylene glycol lowers freezing point of water).
   Blood group testing uses isotonic solutions to prevent cell lysis.

4. OSMOTIC PRESSURE (π):
   
   Osmosis: spontaneous flow of solvent through semipermeable membrane from dilute to concentrated solution.
   Osmotic pressure = minimum pressure needed to stop osmosis.
   
   π = MRT = (n/V)RT
   [M = molarity, R = 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K OR 8.314 J/mol·K, T in Kelvin]
   
   Comparing solutions:
   → Isotonic: same osmotic pressure (same solute concentration)
   → Hypertonic: higher osmotic pressure
   → Hypotonic: lower osmotic pressure
   
   Reverse osmosis: applying pressure greater than π to force solvent from concentrated to dilute (used in water purification).
   
   JEE NOTE: At very low concentrations, osmotic pressure is the most sensitive colligative property for molar mass determination (especially for polymers and proteins).

JEE TRAP: Kb and Kf are properties of the SOLVENT, not the solute. Kb(water) ≠ Kf(water).

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4: VAN'T HOFF FACTOR (i)
----------------------------------------------------------------

van't Hoff factor (i) = actual number of particles / expected number of particles
OR: i = normal molar mass / observed molar mass

For non-electrolytes: i = 1 (no dissociation, no association)
For electrolytes that dissociate: i > 1
   NaCl → Na+ + Cl-: i = 2 (ideal, complete dissociation)
   MgCl2 → Mg2+ + 2Cl-: i = 3
   K2SO4 → 2K+ + SO4²-: i = 3
   Al2(SO4)3 → 2Al3+ + 3SO4²-: i = 5

For solutes that associate: i < 1
   Acetic acid in benzene (forms dimers): i < 1
   i approaches 0.5 for complete dimerization

Modified colligative properties with van't Hoff factor:
   ΔTb = i × Kb × m
   ΔTf = i × Kf × m
   π = i × MRT
   RLVP: (PA° - PA)/PA° = i × χB (approximately)

Degree of dissociation (α) from i:
For AxBy → xA^(y+) + yB^(x-):
   Total particles per formula unit = x + y = n
   i = 1 + α(n-1)
   α = (i-1)/(n-1)

JEE EXAMPLE: For NaCl (n=2): i = 1 + α(2-1) = 1 + α
If i = 1.87, then α = 0.87 (87% dissociation)

JEE TRAP: Van't Hoff factor for strong electrolytes in dilute solution approaches ideal value but is never exactly equal (due to interionic interactions). For calculation purposes in JEE, use ideal i value unless told otherwise.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5: ABNORMAL MOLAR MASS
----------------------------------------------------------------

When solute dissociates or associates, the observed molar mass differs from expected.

Observed M < Expected M → dissociation (i > 1)
Observed M > Expected M → association (i < 1)

Examples:
→ NaCl in water: dissociates → observed M ≈ 29 g/mol (instead of 58.5 g/mol)
→ Acetic acid in benzene: associates into dimers → observed M ≈ 120 g/mol (instead of 60 g/mol)

SECTION 6: JEE-SPECIFIC PATTERNS
----------------------------------------------------------------

HIGH FREQUENCY QUESTION TYPES:
1. Calculate ΔTf or ΔTb given molality or mass data
2. Find molar mass of solute using any colligative property
3. Calculate osmotic pressure and compare solutions (isotonic/hypertonic/hypotonic)
4. Apply van't Hoff factor to modify colligative property
5. Identify positive vs negative deviation from Raoult's law
6. Relative lowering of vapour pressure calculation
7. Convert between concentration units (molarity ↔ molality ↔ mole fraction)

CRITICAL TRAPS:
→ ΔTf = Kf × m (molality, NOT molarity). Temperature doesn't change molality.
→ Kf of water = 1.86, Kb of water = 0.512 — these are different values, don't confuse them.
→ Colligative property depends on NUMBER of particles, not their type. 0.1 m NaCl ≠ 0.1 m glucose in effect (NaCl gives 2 particles, glucose gives 1).
→ Osmotic pressure: π = MRT uses MOLARITY. ΔTb and ΔTf use MOLALITY.
→ Elevation of boiling point and depression of freezing point are always positive values (they represent magnitudes of change). Boiling point goes UP, freezing point goes DOWN.

→ Activity coefficients and deviations from ideal behavior in electrolyte solutions
→ Donnan membrane equilibrium

SECTION 7: KEY FORMULAS SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------

M = mol / L(solution)
m = mol / kg(solvent)
χA = nA/(nA+nB)

PA = χA · PA° (Raoult's Law)
p = KH · χ (Henry's Law)

ΔTb = i · Kb · m
ΔTf = i · Kf · m
π = i · M · R · T
(PA°-PA)/PA° = i · χB

i = 1 + α(n-1), where n = total ions per formula unit
α = (i-1)/(n-1)

Kf(water) = 1.86 K·kg/mol
Kb(water) = 0.512 K·kg/mol
