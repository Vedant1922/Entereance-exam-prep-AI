UNIT 16: HALOALKANES AND HALOARENES
================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------
Nature of C-X Bond
----------------------------------------------------------------

→ C-X bond is polar (X is more electronegative): Cδ+-Xδ-.
→ Bond length: C-F < C-Cl < C-Br < C-I (increases down the group, atomic size increases).
→ Bond strength: C-F > C-Cl > C-Br > C-I (decreases down group).
→ Polarity: C-F most polar, C-I least polar.

→ C-X bond is weaker in haloalkanes than in haloarenes (due to partial double bond character in haloarenes via resonance).

----------------------------------------------------------------
Preparation of Haloalkanes
----------------------------------------------------------------

From alcohols: ROH + PCl5 → RCl + POCl3 + HCl
              ROH + SOCl2 → RCl + SO2 + HCl (preferred, gaseous byproducts escape)
              ROH + HX → RX (Lucas reagent: HCl + ZnCl2)
              
Lucas Test: used to distinguish 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols.
→ 3° alcohol: reacts immediately with Lucas reagent (turbidity instantly).
→ 2° alcohol: turbidity in 5 minutes.
→ 1° alcohol: no turbidity (or reacts very slowly, needs heating).

Halogenation: R-H + X2 → R-X (free radical mechanism, UV light).
Allylic/benzylic halogenation with NBS (N-Bromosuccinimide): 
→ Selectively brominates allylic/benzylic positions (free radical, more stable radical).

Addition to Alkenes: CH2=CH2 + HX → CH3CH2X (Markovnikov).

----------------------------------------------------------------
NUCLEOPHILIC SUBSTITUTION MECHANISMS
----------------------------------------------------------------

SN1 (Unimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution):
→ Two-step mechanism: Step 1 = rate-determining ionization to carbocation. Step 2 = fast nucleophilic attack.
→ Rate = k[RX] (depends only on substrate, NOT nucleophile).
→ Favored by: 3° substrates (stable carbocations), polar protic solvents (stabilize ions).
→ Gives RACEMIZATION (nucleophile attacks carbocation from both faces → 50:50 R and S).
→ Carbocation can rearrange if more stable carbocation forms by 1,2-shift.

SN2 (Bimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution):
→ One-step mechanism: simultaneous bond breaking + bond forming (concerted).
→ Rate = k[RX][Nu] (depends on both substrate and nucleophile).
→ Favored by: 1° substrates (least steric hindrance), strong nucleophiles, polar aprotic solvents (DMSO, acetone, DMF).
→ Gives INVERSION of configuration (Walden inversion — nucleophile attacks from BACK of C-X bond).
→ Backside attack → complete stereochemical inversion (R → S or S → R).

Substrate reactivity:
SN1: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3X (3° forms most stable carbocation)
SN2: CH3X > 1° > 2° > 3° (least steric hindrance at methyl and primary)

Nucleophile strength for SN2: I- > Br- > Cl- > F- (in protic solvents)
(Iodide is the best nucleophile because it's large, polarizable, low solvation)

JEE TRAP: Benzylic and allylic systems: fast SN1 due to resonance-stabilized carbocations AND fast SN2 due to resonance-stabilized transition state. Both mechanisms possible.

Elimination vs Substitution competition:
→ Strong, bulky base → elimination preferred (E2).
→ Weak nucleophile/base, high temperature → elimination.
→ Primary substrate + strong nucleophile → SN2 dominates.
→ Tertiary substrate + strong base → E2 dominates.
→ Tertiary substrate + weak nucleophile + polar protic solvent → SN1/E1.

ELIMINATION (E2):
→ Anti-periplanar requirement: H and X must be anti to each other.
→ Saytzeff's rule: more substituted alkene forms (major product).
→ Zaitsev's rule = Saytzeff's rule.
→ Hofmann's rule: with bulky base, LESS substituted alkene forms.

----------------------------------------------------------------
Environmental Concerns
----------------------------------------------------------------

Chloroform (CHCl3): toxic, carcinogenic, was used as anesthetic.
Iodoform (CHI3): antiseptic, used as wound dressing (iodoform test for methyl ketones).
Freons (CCl2F2 etc.): refrigerants, cause ozone layer depletion (Cl• radical chain reaction).
DDT (dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane): insecticide, now banned (non-biodegradable, accumulates in food chain, endocrine disruptor).

Iodoform test (haloform reaction): positive for CH3COR compounds (methyl ketones) and ethanol.
Reaction: CH3COR + I2 + NaOH → CHI3 (yellow ppt, antiseptic smell) + RCOONa

----------------------------------------------------------------
ALCOHOLS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Classification: Primary (1°, -CH2OH), Secondary (2°, RCHOH), Tertiary (3°, R3COH).

Physical Properties:
→ Higher BP than corresponding alkanes (H-bonding).
→ Lower BP than water (alcohols have one H-bond per molecule vs water's average ~2).
→ Short-chain alcohols are water-miscible (H-bonding with water). Long-chain are insoluble.

Acidity of alcohols:
→ Alcohols are weakly acidic (pKa ≈ 16-18). Weaker than water (pKa ≈ 15.7) for most secondary and tertiary alcohols.
→ Acidity order: 1° > 2° > 3° (alkyl groups increase electron density on O → less acidic).
   CH3OH > RCH2OH > R2CHOH > R3COH
→ Phenol is much more acidic than alcohols (pKa ≈ 10) due to resonance stabilization of phenoxide ion.

Reactions:
1. With sodium metal: 2ROH + 2Na → 2RONa + H2↑ (less vigorous than Na + H2O)
2. Dehydration with conc. H2SO4:
   at 440-450K (170°C): gives alkene (intramolecular elimination) → Saytzeff product (major).
   at 413K (140°C) with excess alcohol: gives ether (intermolecular dehydration) → R-O-R.
3. Oxidation:
   1° alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid (with KMnO4 or K2Cr2O7/H2SO4)
   2° alcohol → ketone (oxidation stops here usually)
   3° alcohol → resistant to oxidation under mild conditions.
   PCC (pyridinium chlorochromate): oxidizes 1° to aldehyde ONLY (does not oxidize further).
4. Esterification: RCOOH + R'OH ⇌ RCOOR' + H2O (acid catalyst, reversible).
5. Lucas Test: HCl + ZnCl2 → 3° turbid immediately, 2° in 5 min, 1° slow/no turbidity.

JEE TRAP: Dehydration gives less water-containing product → alkene favored at higher temperature and lower alcohol concentration. Ether favored at lower temperature and higher alcohol concentration.

----------------------------------------------------------------
PHENOLS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Phenol: C6H5-OH (benzene with -OH attached to ring)

Acidic character:
→ Phenol more acidic than alcohols (pKa ≈ 10 vs ≈ 16 for alcohols).
→ Phenoxide ion stabilized by resonance (negative charge delocalized over ring).
→ Phenol is weakly acidic: reacts with NaOH but NOT with NaHCO3 (unlike carboxylic acids).
→ Carboxylic acids react with NaHCO3, phenols do NOT → used to distinguish them.

Electron-donating OH group → ring activated for EAS → o/p director.

Reactions on Ring (EAS, very reactive compared to benzene):
1. Bromination: PhOH + Br2 (aq) → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white ppt) — no catalyst needed (OH activates ring strongly).
2. Nitration: PhOH + dil HNO3 → mixture of o- and p-nitrophenol.
3. Sulfonation: PhOH + H2SO4 → o-phenolsulfonic acid (at room T) or p-phenolsulfonic acid (at higher T).
4. Reimer-Tiemann Reaction: PhOH + CHCl3 + NaOH → 2-hydroxybenzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde) [CHO at ortho].
5. Kolbe Reaction: PhONa + CO2 (high pressure, 125°C) → sodium salicylate → salicylic acid (aspirin precursor).

Reaction on OH group:
1. Etherification (Williamson synthesis): PhONa + CH3I → PhOCH3 (anisole) + NaI.
2. Esterification: PhOH + (CH3CO)2O → PhOOCCH3 (phenyl acetate) + CH3COOH.

JEE TRAP: Phenol + NaOH → sodium phenoxide (soluble) → confirms phenol's acidic nature.
Phenol + FeCl3 → violet color (characteristic test for phenol).
Phenol + Br2/water → white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol (immediately, no catalyst).

Comparison of acidic strength:
carboxylic acid >> phenol >> H2O >> alcohol
(Carboxylic acid reacts with NaHCO3. Phenol does not. Alcohol does not.)

o-nitrophenol vs p-nitrophenol:
→ o-nitrophenol: intramolecular H-bond with NO2 → lower BP, steam-volatile, less soluble in water.
→ p-nitrophenol: intermolecular H-bond with water molecules → higher BP, more water soluble.

----------------------------------------------------------------
ETHERS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Structure: R-O-R' (two alkyl groups on oxygen)

Preparation: Williamson Synthesis: R-O-Na + R'X → R-O-R' + NaX
→ Best with 1° alkyl halide and sodium alkoxide.
→ SN2 mechanism → 3° RX leads to elimination instead.

Properties:
→ Relatively inert under normal conditions.
→ Used as solvents (diethyl ether, THF, dioxane).
→ Ether oxygen has lone pairs → Lewis base → forms complexes with Lewis acids (BF3, AlCl3).

Reactions:
1. Cleavage by HI (or HBr): R-O-R' + HI → R-OH + R'-I (or R-I + R'-OH depending on R and R').
   If ether is unsymmetrical: smaller or primary group becomes iodide (SN2).
   For mixed ether with 3° alkyl group: SN1 with larger alkyl group as carbocation.
2. Reaction with conc. H2SO4 at high temperature: cleaves ether bond.
3. Peroxide formation: ethers stored in air form explosive peroxides.

JEE TRAP: Test for ether = reaction with conc. HI to give alkyl iodide. Williamson synthesis requires sodium alkoxide (not just alcohol + alkyl halide).

----------------------------------------------------------------
ALDEHYDES AND KETONES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Aldehydes: R-CHO (carbonyl at end of chain). Ketones: R-CO-R' (carbonyl in middle).

Physical Properties:
→ Carbonyl group is polar (C=O) → higher BP than alkanes but lower than alcohols (no H-bonding in aldehydes/ketones).
→ Lower aldehydes/ketones are water soluble (H-bond with water via carbonyl O).

Nucleophilic Addition to Carbonyl (C=O):
→ Carbonyl carbon is electrophilic (C is δ+) → attacked by nucleophiles.
→ Aldehydes are MORE reactive than ketones in nucleophilic addition.
   Reason: (1) Less steric hindrance in aldehyde. (2) Ketone has 2 electron-donating alkyl groups (reduce C electrophilicity). (3) Inductive effect.

KEY NUCLEOPHILIC ADDITION REACTIONS:

1. Addition of HCN (Hydrogen Cyanide):
   RCHO + HCN → RCH(OH)CN (cyanohydrin)
   Mechanism: CN- (nucleophile) attacks C=O → adds CN- → protonation gives cyanohydrin.
   Product has new chiral center if R ≠ H → racemic mixture.
   
2. Addition of NaHSO3 (Sodium Bisulfite):
   RCHO + NaHSO3 → RCHO·NaHSO3 (crystalline adduct) — only aldehydes and methyl ketones.
   Used to purify aldehydes.

3. Addition of NH3 and derivatives (R-NH2, NH2OH, etc.):
   RCHO + NH3 → alkylimine (Schiff base) — unstable.
   RCHO + NH2OH → aldoxime (C=N-OH).
   RCHO + NH2-NH2 → hydrazone (C=N-NH2).
   RCHO + NH2-NH-C6H5 → phenylhydrazone.
   2,4-DNP test: RCHO or RCOR' + 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine → orange/yellow precipitate = confirms C=O.

4. Addition of Grignard Reagent:
   RCHO + R'MgX → secondary alcohol (after hydrolysis).
   RCOR'' + R'MgX → tertiary alcohol.
   HCHO (formaldehyde) + RMgX → primary alcohol.

5. Cannizzaro Reaction:
   Only for aldehydes WITHOUT alpha hydrogen (like HCHO, PhCHO, Cl3CCHO).
   2RCHO + NaOH → RCOONa + RCH2OH (one oxidized, one reduced — disproportionation).

6. Aldol Condensation:
   Only for carbonyl compounds with alpha-H (H adjacent to C=O).
   2CH3CHO + NaOH → CH3CH(OH)CH2CHO (aldol — beta-hydroxy aldehyde) → dehydration → CH3CH=CHCHO (crotonaldehyde, alpha,beta-unsaturated aldehyde).

7. Clemmensen Reduction (Zn-Hg/HCl): 
   C=O → CH2 (reduces carbonyl to methylene). For acid-sensitive compounds.

8. Wolff-Kishner Reduction (N2H4/KOH):
   C=O → CH2. Used with alkaline condition. For acid-sensitive substrates.

9. Tollens' Test (Silver Mirror Test):
   Only ALDEHYDES give positive test. Ketones do NOT.
   RCHO + [Ag(NH3)2]+ (Tollen's reagent) → RCOONa + Ag (mirror on glass).

10. Fehling's Test:
    Only ALDEHYDES give brick-red precipitate (Cu2O). Ketones do NOT.
    Aliphatic aldehydes give positive Fehling's. Benzaldehyde does NOT (aromatic).

11. Iodoform Test (Haloform reaction):
    Positive for: CH3COCH3 (acetone), CH3CHO, ethanol, other methyl ketones, secondary alcohols that can be oxidized to methyl ketone.
    RCOCH3 + I2 + NaOH → CHI3 (yellow ppt, antiseptic smell) + RCOONa.

JEE TRAP: 
→ Toll ens' test: ALL aldehydes (aliphatic and aromatic). Ketones: NEGATIVE.
→ Fehling's test: only ALIPHATIC aldehydes. Benzaldehyde: NEGATIVE. Ketones: NEGATIVE.
→ Iodoform: methyl ketones + ethanol. NOT just ketones in general.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CARBOXYLIC ACIDS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Acidity:
→ Carboxylic acids much more acidic than alcohols (pKa 4-5 vs 16).
→ Carboxylate anion stabilized by resonance (negative charge delocalized over two O atoms).

Factors affecting acid strength:
→ Electron-withdrawing groups (EWG) increase acidity (stabilize carboxylate ion further).
   F3CCOOH (pKa = 0.5) > Cl3CCOOH > CHCl2COOH > ClCH2COOH > CH3COOH (pKa = 4.75)
→ Closer the EWG to COOH, stronger the effect.
→ Electron-donating groups decrease acidity.
→ More EWG substituents → more acidic.

Important acidity comparisons:
→ Formic acid (HCOOH) > Acetic acid (CH3COOH): H is less electron-donating than CH3.
→ Benzoic acid > acetic acid: phenyl ring is electron-withdrawing relative to CH3.
→ p-nitrobenzoic acid > benzoic acid: NO2 is EWG, withdraws electrons.
→ p-methylbenzoic acid < benzoic acid: CH3 is electron-donating.

Reactions:
1. Salt formation: RCOOH + NaOH → RCOONa + H2O.
2. Esterification: RCOOH + R'OH ⇌ RCOOR' + H2O (acid-catalyzed, reversible).
3. Reaction with LiAlH4: reduces -COOH to -CH2OH (primary alcohol).
4. Decarboxylation: removes CO2. Easy for: beta-keto acids, malonic acid derivatives.
   CH3COCH2COOH → CH3COCH3 + CO2 (beta-keto acid decarboxylation).

----------------------------------------------------------------
AMINES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Classification:
Primary (1°): RNH2 (one alkyl/aryl group on N)
Secondary (2°): R2NH (two groups on N)
Tertiary (3°): R3N (three groups on N)
Quaternary ammonium salt: R4N+X- (four groups, no lone pair — not a base)

Basic Character:
→ Amines are Lewis bases (lone pair on N) → accept proton.
→ Basicity: Kb (or pKb = 14 - pKa of conjugate acid).
→ More electron density on N → more basic.

Basicity order in aliphatic amines:
2° > 1° > 3° > NH3 [in gas phase due to inductive effect only]
In water (aqueous): 2° > 1° ≈ 3° > NH3 [hydration stabilizes conjugate acid; 3° is less hydrated]

In aromatic amines:
→ Aniline (C6H5NH2) is MUCH less basic than aliphatic amines.
→ Lone pair on N is delocalized into benzene ring → less available for protonation.
→ pKb of aniline ≈ 9.4, vs ethylamine ≈ 3.4 (aniline is far less basic).

Basicity order:
Aliphatic amine >> NH3 >> aromatic amine (aniline, etc.)

Effect of substituents on aniline's basicity:
→ EWG on ring (like -NO2 at para) → decreases basicity further.
→ EDG on ring (like -CH3 at para) → increases basicity slightly.

Preparation of Amines:
1. Reduction of nitro compounds: ArNO2 + 3Fe + 6HCl → ArNH2 + ... (Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl)
2. Reduction of nitriles: RCN + 4[H] → RCH2NH2 (via LiAlH4)
3. Gabriel phthalimide synthesis: makes primary amines free of secondary and tertiary.
4. Hofmann bromamide reaction: RCONH2 + Br2 + 4NaOH → RNH2 + Na2CO3 + 2NaBr + 2H2O
   [One carbon less than starting amide — useful for lower primary amine]

Reactions:
1. With HCl: RNH2 + HCl → RNH3+Cl- (salt formation — amines are bases).
2. With acyl chloride: RNH2 + R'COCl → R'CONHR + HCl (amide formation).
3. With NaNO2 + HCl:
   Aliphatic 1° amines: unstable diazonium salt → N2 gas + alcohol.
   Aromatic 1° amines: stable diazonium salt ArN2+Cl- (diazotization — at 0-5°C).

Distinction tests:
→ Primary amine + CHCl3 + KOH (alcoholic) → isocyanide (bad-smelling) — carbylamine test. ONLY primary amines.
→ Hinsberg test: Primary amine reacts with benzenesulfonyl chloride (PhSO2Cl) → sulfonamide soluble in NaOH. Secondary amine → sulfonamide insoluble in NaOH. Tertiary → no reaction.

Hofmann's exhaustive methylation: converts amine to quaternary salt → Hofmann elimination → least substituted alkene.
(Opposite of Saytzeff's rule — gives less substituted alkene.)

JEE TRAP: Carbylamine test is ONLY positive for PRIMARY amines (not 2° or 3°). Bad smell = isocyanide = positive test.

----------------------------------------------------------------
DIAZONIUM SALTS
----------------------------------------------------------------

Formation: Ar-NH2 + NaNO2 + HCl (0-5°C) → Ar-N2+Cl- (aryldiazonium salt) + 2H2O

Stability: ArN2+ is stable at 0-5°C due to resonance (nitrogen stabilized by aryl ring).
Aliphatic diazonium salts are unstable → lose N2 immediately.

REACTIONS OF DIAZONIUM SALTS (very important for JEE — synthetic route):

REPLACEMENT OF N2 by other groups:
→ Ar-N2+Cl- + CuCl → Ar-Cl + N2 (Sandmeyer reaction with Cu salts)
→ Ar-N2+Cl- + CuBr → Ar-Br + N2 (Sandmeyer)
→ Ar-N2+Cl- + CuCN → Ar-CN + N2 (Sandmeyer)
→ Ar-N2+Cl- + HBF4 → Ar-F + N2 (Balz-Schiemann reaction, heat)
→ Ar-N2+Cl- + KI → Ar-I + N2 + KCl (Finkelstein type — no Cu needed for I)
→ Ar-N2+Cl- + H2O (heat, dilute H2SO4) → Ar-OH + N2 (phenol formation)
→ Ar-N2+Cl- + H3PO2/H2O → Ar-H + N2 (removal of -NH2 group, replacing with H)
→ Ar-N2+Cl- + conc. HBF4 → ArN2BF4 → heat → Ar-F + N2 + BF3 (Schiemann)

COUPLING REACTIONS (electrophilic attack on activated aromatic ring):
→ Ar-N2+Cl- + aniline (in acid) → Ar-N=N-Ar' (azo compound, orange/yellow dye)
→ Ar-N2+Cl- + phenol (in alkaline) → Ar-N=N-Ar'-OH (azo compound, orange dye)
→ Diazonium couples at p-position (or o- if p is blocked) of activated ring.
→ Azo dyes — commercially important.

JEE APPLICATIONS:
→ Diazonium salts allow conversion of -NH2 to virtually any functional group (via N2 as leaving group).
→ Starting from aniline → can access: halides, cyanide, phenol, fluoride, which are not easy to make otherwise.

JEE TRAP: Sandmeyer reaction uses CUPROUS salts (Cu+, CuCl, CuBr). NOT cupric.
Replacement by I does NOT need Cu catalyst (I- attacks directly on N2 complex).

HIGH FREQUENCY QUESTION TYPES:
1. Predict SN1 vs SN2, identify major product of nucleophilic substitution
2. Identify positive/negative result for Tollens', Fehling's, iodoform tests
3. Write product of aldol condensation or Cannizzaro reaction
4. Compare basicity/acidity of given compounds
5. Write product of diazonium salt reaction (Sandmeyer, coupling)
6. Identify the transformation in given synthesis: which reactants/conditions used
7. Identify 1°/2°/3° alcohol from reaction behavior (Lucas test, oxidation)
8. Write product of esterification, dehydration of alcohol, or ester hydrolysis

CRITICAL TRAPS:
→ Tollens' test: ALL aldehydes including aromatic. Fehling's: only ALIPHATIC aldehydes.
→ Iodoform test: only methyl ketones (CH3COR) and ethanol.
→ Cannizzaro: only for aldehydes WITHOUT alpha-H (no CH adjacent to CHO).
→ Aldol condensation: only for carbonyl compounds WITH alpha-H.
→ SN1: 3° substrates, protic solvents, weak nucleophile. SN2: 1° substrates, aprotic solvents, strong nucleophile.
→ Diazonium stability: aryl > alkyl. Must keep at 0-5°C.
→ Aniline much less basic than aliphatic amines (lone pair delocalized in ring).
→ Carbylamine test is ONLY for primary amines.
→ p-nitrophenol more acidic than phenol more acidic than alcohol more acidic than water (comparing pKa values).
