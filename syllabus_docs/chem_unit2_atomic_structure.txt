SECTION 1: SUBATOMIC PARTICLES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Electron: charge = -1.6 × 10^-19 C, mass = 9.1 × 10^-31 kg (negligible in mass calculations)
Proton: charge = +1.6 × 10^-19 C, mass = 1.67 × 10^-27 kg
Neutron: charge = 0, mass = 1.67 × 10^-27 kg

Atomic Number (Z) = number of protons = number of electrons (in neutral atom)
Mass Number (A) = protons + neutrons
Neutrons = A - Z

Isotopes: Same Z, different A (same element, different mass number)
Example: C-12 and C-14 are isotopes of carbon.
Isobars: Same A, different Z (different elements, same mass number)
Isotones: Same number of neutrons, different Z

JEE TRAP: In a cation (positive ion), electrons are removed. In anion, electrons are added.
Fe2+ has 26 - 2 = 24 electrons. O2- has 8 + 2 = 10 electrons.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: ATOMIC MODELS — EVOLUTION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Thomson's Model (Plum Pudding):
→ Atom = positive sphere with electrons embedded in it.
→ Failed because: could not explain Rutherford's gold foil experiment.

Rutherford's Nuclear Model:
→ Gold foil experiment: alpha particles fired at thin gold foil.
→ Most passed through → atom is mostly empty space.
→ Few deflected at large angles → small, dense, positive nucleus exists.
→ Very few bounced back → nucleus is extremely hard and small.
→ Limitations: Could not explain atomic stability (accelerating electrons should radiate energy and spiral into nucleus), and could not explain discrete atomic spectra.

Bohr's Model (Valid ONLY for hydrogen and hydrogen-like ions: He+, Li2+, Be3+):
Postulates:
1. Electrons move in fixed circular orbits (shells) without radiating energy.
2. Each orbit has fixed energy — energy is quantized.
3. Energy is emitted or absorbed only when electron jumps between orbits.
4. Angular momentum of electron is quantized: mvr = nh/2π

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3: BOHR MODEL — KEY EQUATIONS (Very High JEE Frequency)
----------------------------------------------------------------

For hydrogen-like species (charge Z on nucleus):

Energy of nth orbit:
   En = -13.6 × Z² / n² eV
   En = -2.18 × 10^-18 × Z² / n² J

Radius of nth orbit:
   rn = 0.529 × n² / Z Å (angstroms)
   rn = 52.9 × n² / Z pm (picometers)

Velocity of electron in nth orbit:
   vn = 2.18 × 10^6 × Z / n m/s

For hydrogen (Z=1):
   E1 = -13.6 eV (ground state energy)
   r1 = 0.529 Å (Bohr radius)

Energy of photon emitted/absorbed during transition:
   ΔE = Efinal - Einitial = 13.6 × Z² × (1/n1² - 1/n2²) eV
   [n2 > n1 for emission, ΔE is positive for absorption]

Wavenumber (Rydberg equation):
   1/λ = RH × Z² × (1/n1² - 1/n2²)
   RH = 1.097 × 10^7 m^-1 (Rydberg constant)
   c = νλ, E = hν = hc/λ

SPECTRAL SERIES OF HYDROGEN — MUST MEMORIZE:
   Lyman series:    n1=1, n2=2,3,4... → Ultraviolet region
   Balmer series:   n1=2, n2=3,4,5... → Visible region (most important for JEE)
   Paschen series:  n1=3, n2=4,5,6... → Infrared
   Brackett series: n1=4, n2=5,6,7... → Infrared
   Pfund series:    n1=5, n2=6,7,8... → Far Infrared

Number of spectral lines when electron falls from nth to ground state = n(n-1)/2

JEE TRAP: Lyman series is UV, Balmer is visible. Students often confuse these.
JEE TRAP: The first line of Balmer (n=3 to n=2) is in visible — called H-alpha line (red).

Limitations of Bohr's Model:
→ Only works for hydrogen-like species (1 electron). Fails for multi-electron atoms.
→ Cannot explain Zeeman effect (splitting of spectral lines in magnetic field).
→ Cannot explain fine structure of spectral lines.
→ Does not explain chemical bonding.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4: DUAL NATURE OF MATTER AND RADIATION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Wave Nature of Light:
→ Light exhibits interference and diffraction — proves wave nature.
→ λ = c/ν, E = hν = hc/λ
→ h = Planck's constant = 6.626 × 10^-34 J·s

Photoelectric Effect (Einstein's explanation):
→ Light shining on metal surface ejects electrons.
→ KE of emitted electron = hν - hν0 = hν - φ (work function)
→ Threshold frequency (ν0): minimum frequency needed to eject electron.
→ KEY: Increasing intensity increases NUMBER of electrons, not their KE.
→ Increasing frequency increases KE of electrons, not their number.
→ This proved particle nature of light (photons).

JEE TRAP: Below threshold frequency, NO electrons are ejected regardless of intensity. This is one of the most tested concepts.

de Broglie Wavelength (Wave nature of matter):
→ Every moving particle has an associated wavelength.
   λ = h/mv = h/p
   where m = mass, v = velocity, p = momentum

→ For electron accelerated through potential difference V:
   λ = h / √(2meV) = 12.27/√V Å (V in volts)

Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle:
   Δx × Δp ≥ h/4π
   Δx × (mΔv) ≥ h/4π

→ Cannot simultaneously know exact position AND exact momentum of a particle.
→ This is WHY Bohr's model is fundamentally flawed — it assumed fixed orbits with known position and velocity simultaneously.

JEE APPLICATION: If Δx is given, find minimum Δv = h/(4π × m × Δx)

SECTION 5: QUANTUM MECHANICAL MODEL
----------------------------------------------------------------

Orbital = region of space where probability of finding electron is maximum (≥90%).
NOT a fixed path. It is a probability distribution (electron cloud).

Atomic orbital is described by a wave function ψ.
|ψ|² = probability density of finding electron at a point.

FOUR QUANTUM NUMBERS — define the state of an electron completely:

1. Principal Quantum Number (n):
   → Values: 1, 2, 3, 4... (positive integers only)
   → Determines energy (primarily) and size of orbital.
   → Higher n = higher energy = larger orbital.
   → Maximum electrons in shell = 2n²

2. Azimuthal/Angular Momentum Quantum Number (l):
   → Values: 0 to (n-1)
   → Determines shape of orbital.
   → l=0 → s orbital (spherical)
   → l=1 → p orbital (dumbbell)
   → l=2 → d orbital (cloverleaf/complex)
   → l=3 → f orbital
   → Number of orbitals in a subshell = 2l+1

3. Magnetic Quantum Number (ml):
   → Values: -l to +l (including 0), total = 2l+1 values
   → Determines orientation of orbital in space.
   → s: ml=0 (1 orbital), p: ml=-1,0,+1 (3 orbitals), d: ml=-2,-1,0,+1,+2 (5 orbitals)

4. Spin Quantum Number (ms):
   → Values: +1/2 or -1/2 only
   → Represents intrinsic angular momentum (spin) of electron.
   → Two electrons in same orbital have opposite spins.

JEE TRAP: A valid set of quantum numbers must satisfy ALL rules simultaneously.
Common invalid sets:
   n=2, l=2 → INVALID (l must be 0 to n-1, so max l=1 for n=2)
   n=3, l=1, ml=2 → INVALID (ml must be -1 to +1 for l=1)
   ms=0 → INVALID (ms can only be +1/2 or -1/2)

SHAPES OF ORBITALS:
   s orbital: Spherical, one lobe, non-directional. Increases in size with n.
   p orbital: Two lobes on opposite sides of nucleus along an axis. Three orientations: px, py, pz.
   d orbital: Five orbitals. Four cloverleaf-shaped (dxy, dxz, dyz, dx²-y²) + one dz² (dumbbell with ring).
   Node: Region where ψ² = 0 (zero probability). Number of radial nodes = n - l - 1.

SECTION 6: ELECTRONIC CONFIGURATION
----------------------------------------------------------------

THREE RULES (all three must be applied together):

1. Aufbau Principle:
   → Fill orbitals in order of increasing energy.
   → Energy order: 1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p...
   → Use (n+l) rule: Lower (n+l) = filled first. If (n+l) same, lower n = filled first.
   → 4s (n+l=4) fills before 3d (n+l=5) ← CRITICAL

2. Pauli Exclusion Principle:
   → No two electrons in an atom can have all four quantum numbers identical.
   → Maximum 2 electrons per orbital, and they must have opposite spins.

3. Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity:
   → Electrons fill degenerate orbitals (same energy) one each before pairing.
   → All singly occupied orbitals have same spin (all ↑ before any ↓).

CRITICAL EXCEPTIONS — JEE TESTS THESE EVERY YEAR:

Chromium (Z=24): Expected: [Ar] 4s² 3d⁴ → Actual: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵
Reason: Half-filled 3d (3d⁵) gives extra stability due to symmetrical distribution.

Copper (Z=29): Expected: [Ar] 4s² 3d⁹ → Actual: [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰
Reason: Completely filled 3d (3d¹⁰) gives extra stability.

→ RULE: Half-filled and completely filled subshells (d⁵, d¹⁰, f⁷, f¹⁴) have extra stability.
→ These exceptions occur because the energy gained from extra stability exceeds the energy cost of promoting one electron from 4s to 3d.

JEE TRAP: When writing electronic configuration of ions, remove electrons from 4s before 3d.
Fe: [Ar] 4s² 3d⁶ → Fe²⁺: [Ar] 3d⁶ (both 4s electrons removed first)
→ Fe²⁺ is NOT [Ar] 4s² 3d⁴. Students often make this error.

Number of orbitals in a shell n = n²
Number of electrons maximum in shell n = 2n²

SECTION 7: ELECTROMAGNETIC RADIATION
----------------------------------------------------------------

Nature of EMR:
→ Transverse waves consisting of oscillating electric and magnetic fields.
→ Travel at speed of light c = 3 × 10^8 m/s in vacuum.
→ c = νλ (c = frequency × wavelength)

Electromagnetic Spectrum (increasing frequency / energy):
Radio < Microwave < Infrared < Visible < UV < X-rays < Gamma rays

Visible light wavelength range: 400nm (violet) to 700nm (red)
Energy: E = hν = hc/λ
Higher frequency = higher energy = shorter wavelength
