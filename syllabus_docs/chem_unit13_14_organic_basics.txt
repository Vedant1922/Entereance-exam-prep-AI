SECTION 1: PURIFICATION METHODS
----------------------------------------------------------------

1. Crystallization:
→ Used for solids that are more soluble at higher temperatures.
→ Dissolve in hot solvent → cool slowly → pure crystals form → filter.
→ Used for: common salt, alum, benzoic acid.

2. Sublimation:
→ Substance converts directly solid → gas without going through liquid (and vice versa).
→ Used for: iodine, camphor, naphthalene, ammonium chloride.
→ Non-sublimable impurities remain behind.

3. Distillation:
→ Simple distillation: liquids with widely different boiling points.
→ Fractional distillation: liquids with close boiling points (petroleum refining, ethanol-water).
→ Distillation under reduced pressure (vacuum distillation): liquids that decompose at their normal boiling points.
→ Steam distillation: purifying immiscible liquids that are steam-volatile at <100°C (aniline, nitrobenzene).

4. Differential Extraction:
→ Separates compound between two immiscible solvents (usually organic + water).
→ Compound partitions preferentially into solvent in which it is more soluble.
→ Multiple extractions with smaller portions more efficient than one large extraction.

5. Chromatography (all types same basic principle: differential migration in mobile vs stationary phase):
→ Column Chromatography: stationary = silica/alumina; mobile = organic solvent flowing through.
→ TLC (Thin Layer Chromatography): stationary = silica on glass plate; mobile = solvent runs up.
   Rf = distance traveled by compound / distance traveled by solvent front. 0 < Rf < 1.
→ Paper Chromatography: stationary = paper (cellulose-water); mobile = solvent. Used for amino acids, sugars.
→ Gas Chromatography (GC): volatile compounds separated by inert gas carrier.

JEE TRAP: Rf value is characteristic of compound + solvent combination. Higher Rf = less polar compound (moves farther with nonpolar mobile phase, less retained by polar stationary phase).

QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS (Detection of Elements):

Lassaigne's Test (Sodium Fusion Test):
→ Fuse sample with Na metal → converts heteroatoms to ionic forms in melt.
   N → NaCN, S → Na2S, Halogen → NaX, P → Na3PO4
→ Dissolve fusion mass in water → filter (gives Lassaigne's extract).

Tests on Lassaigne's extract:
Nitrogen: Add FeSO4 + FeCl3 + dil H2SO4 → Prussian blue precipitate [Fe4(Fe(CN)6)3] confirms N.
Sulfur: Add dilute HCl + lead acetate solution → black ppt (PbS) confirms S.
   OR: Add Na2[Fe(CN)5NO] (sodium nitroprusside) → violet/purple color confirms S.
Halogens: Acidify with HNO3, then add AgNO3:
   AgCl: white ppt, soluble in NH3 → Cl
   AgBr: pale yellow ppt, slightly soluble in NH3 → Br
   AgI: yellow ppt, insoluble in NH3 → I

JEE TRAP: If N and S both present in compound, NaSCN (sodium thiocyanate) forms in fusion. It interferes with nitrogen test (gives blood-red Fe(SCN)3 instead of Prussian blue). Must boil to decompose SCN- before testing for N in this case.

Phosphorus Detection: Fusion extract + ammonium molybdate → yellow ppt [(NH4)3PO4·12MoO3] confirms P.

SECTION 2: TETRAVALENCY AND HYBRIDIZATION OF CARBON
----------------------------------------------------------------

Carbon has 4 valence electrons → can form 4 covalent bonds.
Carbon is unique: can bond with itself (catenation) and form chains, rings, branches.

Hybridization determines geometry and bonding:

sp³ Carbon:
→ 4 sigma bonds, no pi bonds, tetrahedral (109.5°)
→ Examples: alkanes, -CH3, -CH2-, -CH<, >C<
→ C-H bond length: 1.09 Å

sp² Carbon:
→ 3 sigma bonds + 1 pi bond, trigonal planar (120°)
→ Present in: alkenes (C=C), aldehydes/ketones (C=O), carboxylic acids, aromatic rings
→ Shorter C=C: 1.34 Å (double bond)

sp Carbon:
→ 2 sigma bonds + 2 pi bonds, linear (180°)
→ Present in: alkynes (C≡C), nitriles (C≡N)
→ Shortest C≡C: 1.20 Å

Key pattern: more s-character → shorter bond, stronger bond, more acidic C-H.
s-character: sp³=25%, sp²=33%, sp=50%
Acidity order: alkyne H > alkene H > alkane H (C-H bond)

SECTION 3: FUNCTIONAL GROUPS AND HOMOLOGOUS SERIES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Functional group: atom or group of atoms responsible for characteristic properties.

Major Functional Groups for JEE:
-OH (hydroxyl): alcohols, phenols
C=O (carbonyl): aldehydes (-CHO), ketones (>C=O)
-COOH (carboxyl): carboxylic acids
-NH2 (amino): amines
-NO2 (nitro): nitro compounds
-X (halo): halides (F, Cl, Br, I)
-C≡N (cyano/nitrile)
-SO3H (sulfonyl): sulfonic acids
C=C: alkenes
C≡C: alkynes

Homologous Series:
→ Series of compounds with same functional group and general formula.
→ Each member differs by -CH2- from the next.
→ Same general formula, general preparation, general reactions.
→ Physical properties change gradually (BP, MP increase with chain length).

General formulas:
Alkanes: CnH2n+2
Alkenes: CnH2n
Alkynes: CnH2n-2
Benzene series: CnH2n-6
Alcohols: CnH2n+2O (primary, secondary, tertiary)
Aldehydes: CnH2nO
Ketones: CnH2nO
Carboxylic acids: CnH2nO2

SECTION 4: ISOMERISM
----------------------------------------------------------------

Isomers: compounds with SAME molecular formula but DIFFERENT arrangement of atoms.

STRUCTURAL ISOMERISM (constitutional isomerism):

1. Chain (Skeletal) Isomerism: different carbon skeleton.
   n-butane and isobutane (2-methylpropane) → C4H10

2. Position Isomerism: functional group or substituent at different positions.
   1-propanol and 2-propanol → C3H8O

3. Functional Group Isomerism: different functional group, same molecular formula.
   Ethanol (C2H5OH) and dimethyl ether (CH3-O-CH3) → C2H6O
   Ethanoic acid and methyl formate → C2H4O2
   Aldehydes and ketones with same formula: propanal and acetone → C3H6O

4. Metamerism: different alkyl groups on same side of functional group.
   Diethyl ether (C2H5-O-C2H5) and methyl propyl ether (CH3-O-C3H7) → C4H10O
   [Only possible with divalent functional groups: ether, ketone, ester]

5. Tautomerism: rapid interconversion between isomers.
   Keto-enol tautomerism: CH3COCH3 ⇌ CH2=C(OH)CH3 (keto favored)
   
JEE TRAP: Tautomers are NOT true isomers (they interconvert rapidly). But the question may ask about the more stable tautomer.

STEREOISOMERISM (same connectivity, different spatial arrangement):

1. Geometrical Isomerism (cis-trans):
   Condition: restricted rotation around a bond (C=C or ring) AND each carbon has 2 different groups.
   
   Cis: same groups on same side. Trans: same groups on opposite sides.
   
   E-Z nomenclature (modern):
   Assign priority by CIP rules (higher atomic number = higher priority).
   Z (zusammen = together): higher priority groups on same side.
   E (entgegen = opposite): higher priority groups on opposite sides.
   
   JEE: 2-butene has cis and trans isomers. But 2-methylpropene does NOT (one carbon has two identical groups).

2. Optical Isomerism (enantiomers):
   Condition: asymmetric/chiral carbon — carbon bonded to 4 different groups.
   Enantiomers: mirror images, non-superimposable.
   Rotate plane-polarized light: d (+) or l (-) form.
   Racemic mixture: equal amounts of d and l → optically inactive (net rotation = 0).
   
   Diastereomers: stereoisomers that are NOT mirror images (includes meso forms).
   
   Meso compound: has chiral centers but is optically inactive due to internal plane of symmetry.
   Example: meso-tartaric acid (2,3-dihydroxybutanedioic acid with R,S configuration).

JEE TRAP: Number of stereoisomers = 2^n where n = number of chiral carbons (maximum, may be less if meso forms exist).

SECTION 5: IUPAC NOMENCLATURE — KEY RULES
----------------------------------------------------------------

IUPAC Steps:
1. Find longest carbon chain containing functional group (principal chain).
2. Number chain to give functional group lowest possible number.
3. Name substituents as prefixes (methyl, ethyl, chloro, bromo, hydroxy, nitro, amino).
4. If two possible numbering giving same locant for FG, give lowest number to substituent.
5. Use alphabetical order for substituents in name.

Priority order for principal group (determines suffix):
-COOH (acid) > -COO- (ester) > -COX (acid halide) > -CONH2 (amide) > -CHO (aldehyde) > -OH (as "ol") > -NH2 (as "amine") > C=C (as "ene") > C≡C (as "yne")

Lower priority groups become prefixes: -OH → hydroxy, -NH2 → amino, =O → oxo, -X → halo.

Common name to IUPAC (frequently tested):
Acetaldehyde → ethanal
Formaldehyde → methanal
Acetone → propan-2-one
Acetic acid → ethanoic acid
Formic acid → methanoic acid
Benzaldehyde → phenylmethanal (or benzaldehyde retained as IUPAC)
Vinyl/Vinylidene: ethenyl/ethenylidene
Allyl: prop-2-en-1-yl
Phenyl: C6H5- (use as substituent)

SECTION 6: BOND FISSION AND REACTIVE INTERMEDIATES
----------------------------------------------------------------

HOMOLYTIC FISSION (Homolysis):
→ Bond breaks symmetrically — each atom gets one electron.
→ A:B → A• + B•
→ Produces FREE RADICALS (neutral, one unpaired electron).
→ Occurs under UV light, high temperature, or with radical initiators (peroxides).
→ Free radical reactions are CHAIN reactions (initiation, propagation, termination).

HETEROLYTIC FISSION (Heterolysis):
→ Bond breaks asymmetrically — both electrons go to one atom.
→ A:B → A+ + :B- OR A:B → :A- + B+
→ Produces IONS (carbocation, carbanion, or other ions).
→ Occurs in polar conditions (polar solvents, ionic reagents).

REACTIVE INTERMEDIATES:

Carbocation (Carbenium ion):
→ Carbon with positive charge, 3 bonds, empty orbital, sp² hybridized, planar.
→ Stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3+ (primary least stable)
→ Tertiary carbocation most stable because 3 alkyl groups donate electrons through hyperconjugation and inductive effect.
→ Benzylic and allylic carbocations are very stable (delocalization by resonance).

Carbanion:
→ Carbon with negative charge, 3 bonds + lone pair, sp³ hybridized.
→ Stability: CH3- > 1° > 2° > 3° (opposite of carbocations)
→ More electronegative carbon → more stable carbanion.
→ Alkynyl carbanions (sp carbon) are more stable than alkyl carbanions.

Free Radicals:
→ Carbon with one unpaired electron, sp² hybridized, planar.
→ Stability: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH3• (similar to carbocations due to hyperconjugation).
→ Benzylic and allylic radicals are very stable (resonance stabilization).

Carbene (:CH2):
→ Carbon with 2 bonds + 2 non-bonding electrons, very reactive.
→ Not commonly tested in JEE but know the concept.

JEE TRAP: Stability order for carbocations AND free radicals: 3° > 2° > 1° (hyperconjugation + inductive).
But carbanions: 3° < 2° < 1° (electron-donating alkyl groups DESTABILIZE carbanions).

SECTION 7: ELECTRONIC EFFECTS
----------------------------------------------------------------

1. INDUCTIVE EFFECT (I):
→ Transmission of electronic effect through sigma bonds.
→ Sigma electrons are polarized toward more electronegative atom.
→ Fades rapidly with distance (significant only over 2-3 bonds).

Electron-withdrawing groups (-I effect, decrease electron density at attached carbon):
-F > -OH > -Cl > -Br > -I > -COOH > -CHO > -CN > -NO2

Electron-donating groups (+I effect, increase electron density):
-C(CH3)3 > -CH(CH3)2 > -C2H5 > -CH3 > H

JEE APPLICATION:
→ Stability of carboxylic acids: more electronegative substituents increase acid strength.
   CH3COOH < FCH2COOH < F2CHCOOH < F3CCOOH (increasing acidity)

2. RESONANCE EFFECT (R or M — mesomeric effect):
→ Delocalization of electrons through conjugated system (alternating single and multiple bonds).
→ Stabilizes molecules and ions.
→ +R (electron-donating by resonance): -OH, -NH2, -OR, -Cl, -Br (lone pairs donated into ring)
→ -R (electron-withdrawing by resonance): -NO2, -CHO, -COOH, -CN (withdraw π electrons)

In benzene ring:
→ +R groups (OH, NH2): increase electron density at o and p positions → o/p directors in EAS.
→ -R groups (NO2, COOH): decrease electron density at o and p, relative increase at m → m directors.

JEE TRAP: Halogens have both -I (electron-withdrawing inductive) and +R (electron-donating resonance) effects. Overall: they are o/p directors (resonance wins for directing) but deactivating (inductive effect reduces overall ring electron density).

3. HYPERCONJUGATION:
→ Donation of σ (C-H) bonding electrons into adjacent empty p-orbital or π system.
→ Requires C-H bonds adjacent to sp² carbon or carbocation.
→ More adjacent C-H bonds → more hyperconjugation → more stable.
→ Explains why 3° carbocation > 2° > 1° (3° has 9 C-H bonds for hyperconjugation, 2° has 6, 1° has 3).
→ Also explains stability of alkenes: more substituted alkene is more stable (more hyperconjugation).
→ "No bond resonance" — another name for hyperconjugation.

4. ELECTROMERIC EFFECT:
→ Complete transfer of electrons at request of attacking reagent (in reaction).
→ Not a permanent effect (unlike inductive) — only during reaction.
→ +E: electrons shift TOWARD attacking reagent. -E: electrons shift away.
→ Less commonly tested than other effects.

SECTION 8: COMMON REACTION TYPES
----------------------------------------------------------------

Substitution:
→ One atom/group replaced by another.
→ SN1 (unimolecular), SN2 (bimolecular) — covered in haloalkane chapter.
→ Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS): aromatic ring acts as nucleophile.

Addition:
→ Two reactants combine to form one product.
→ Electrophilic addition to alkenes (common).
→ Nucleophilic addition to carbonyl compounds (C=O).

Elimination:
→ Two adjacent atoms/groups removed to form double bond (or triple bond).
→ Dehydrohalogenation, dehydration.
→ Saytzeff's rule: preferentially forms more substituted alkene.

Rearrangement:
→ Atoms reorganize within molecule.
→ Carbocation rearrangements: 1,2-hydride shift or 1,2-methyl shift (from less stable to more stable carbocation).

JEE TRAP: Rearrangement occurs when it leads to a MORE STABLE carbocation. If rearrangement would give less stable carbocation, it doesn't happen.

SECTION 9: JEE-SPECIFIC PATTERNS
----------------------------------------------------------------

HIGH FREQUENCY QUESTION TYPES:
1. Write IUPAC name from structure or structure from IUPAC name
2. Identify number of chiral centers, predict optical isomers
3. Identify functional group isomers for given molecular formula
4. Compare stability of carbocations/free radicals/carbanions
5. Predict whether geometrical isomerism is possible for given structure
6. Identify directing effect of substituent in aromatic compound
7. Identify type of electronic effect (I, R, hyperconjugation) and its direction
8. Detect elements using Lassaigne's test

CRITICAL TRAPS:
→ Meso compounds have chiral centers but are optically inactive (internal mirror plane).
→ Carbanion stability is OPPOSITE to carbocation stability.
→ Alkynes are more acidic than alkenes (sp carbon → more s-character → more electronegative → better stabilizes negative charge on C).
→ For geometrical isomerism: BOTH carbons of double bond must have 2 DIFFERENT groups each. CH2=CHCl has no geometric isomers (one carbon has 2 H's).
→ Hyperconjugation increases stability of carbocations, radicals, AND more substituted alkenes.
→ Halogens in benzene are o/p directors despite being deactivating groups.
