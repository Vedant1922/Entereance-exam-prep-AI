CHAPTER 1: SOME BASIC CONCEPTS OF CHEMISTRY
(JEE FOCUS)
Topics Covered:

Nature of matter

Properties of matter (mass, volume, density)

Laws of chemical combination

Dalton’s atomic theory

Atomic mass & molecular mass

Mole concept & Avogadro number

Molar mass & molar volume

Percentage composition

Empirical formula & molecular formula

Stoichiometry & limiting reagent
Core Concepts:

Matter → has mass + occupies space

Density = mass/volume

Conservation of mass → total mass constant

Definite proportions → fixed mass ratio

Multiple proportions → simple whole number ratios

Atomic mass → relative to 1/12 of C-12

Molecular mass → sum of atomic masses

Mole → 6.022 × 10^23 entities

Molar mass → mass of 1 mole (g/mol)

Avogadro number links particles ↔ moles

Molar volume (gas, STP) → 22.4 L/mol

% composition → fraction of element in compound

Empirical formula → simplest ratio

Molecular formula → actual ratio

Stoichiometry → quantitative relation in reaction

Limiting reagent → reactant consumed first

Excess reagent → remains after reaction
Key Formulas:

Mole = given mass / molar mass

Particles = moles × NA

NA = 6.022 × 10^23

Density = mass/volume

% composition = (mass of element / molar mass) × 100

Empirical formula → mole ratio (simplest)

n = molecular mass / empirical mass

Molecular formula = (Empirical formula)^n

Gas volume (STP) = moles × 22.4 L
Important Patterns:

Always convert → mass → moles → ratio

Assume 100 g in % composition problems

Divide by smallest mole value for ratios


Decimal ratio → multiply to nearest integer

Limiting reagent → compare mole ratio, not mass

Balanced equation → mandatory before solving

Gas problems → check STP condition

Units consistency → g, mol, L alignment
Common Mistakes:

Direct mass ratio instead of mole ratio

Skipping balancing of equation

Ignoring limiting reagent

Wrong molar mass calculation

Not converting units properly

Rounding empirical ratios incorrectly

Confusing empirical vs molecular formula

Using 22.4 L when not at STP
Examples (Important Cases):

% composition of H2O, CO2

Empirical formula from % data (CH2O type)

Molecular formula using molar mass

Limiting reagent in H2 + O2 → H2O

Mole ↔ particles conversion
Exam Tips:

Start with given → find moles → apply ratio

Write units at every step

Memorize atomic masses (H, C, O, N)

Use shortcut → 1 mole gas = 22.4 L (STP only)

Practice limiting reagent (high weight)

Check final answer logically (sanity check)

Avoid skipping intermediate steps

Core Concepts:















Kc = [products]/[reactants]

Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn

pH = −log[H+]

pOH = −log[OH−]

pH + pOH = 14

Ksp = product of ion concentrations

